§ 832. *The object of roots implying motion ( i. e. the place to which the motion is directed ), if it be not a road, is put in the Accusative or the Dative, when actual motion is meant; @[grāmaṃ bhūmaye vā gacchati]@ 'goes to a village;' but @[manasā grāmaṃ vraḍati]@, @[panthānaṃ gacchati]@.

The Ablative Case.

§ 833. The principal sense of the Ablative case is @[apādāna]@ or 'motion away from ;' hence the noun from which the motion, real or conceived, takes place is put in the Ablative case; @[grāmādāyāti]@ 'comes from a village,' @[dhāvamānādaśvātpatati]@ 'falls down from a running horse'; @[sadācalātrādvyatati]@.

(a) { Words having the sense of @[dveṣa]@, @[virāma]@ or 'cessation,' 'pause,' and @[pramāda]@ 'swerving from,' also govern the Ablative case; @[pāpāddveṣṭi]@ 'he hates sin' @[na navaḥ pṛthvīphaladyātasthirakarmā virāma karmaṇaḥ]@ (Ragh. VIII. 22.) the new monarch, steady in action, did not desist from efforts until they bore their fruit'; @[dharmātpramādyati]@ 'swerves from duty'; @[svakāryātsmaṭṭaḥ]@ (Megh. I.) 'careless, failing in the discharge of the duties (of his office )'; so @[dharmāddhyāyati]@; @[prasamīkṣya nivarteta sarvaṃ māṃsasya bhakṣaṇāt]@ (Manu Sm. V. 49.); &c.
