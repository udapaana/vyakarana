512 SANSKRIT GRAMMAR. [ § 895-900
THE PERFECT PARTICIPLES.

§ 895. The use of the perfect participles is very limited; it
is used in the sense of 'who or what has done &c.'; @[taṃ tasthivāṃsaṃ
nagaropakaṇṭhe] (Ragh. 4. 61.) him who had halted in the vicinity
of the city; @[agraṇīḥ sarvāṇyapijagṛbhusaste] (Ragh. 1. 34.) of thee who
hast obtained all good things, @[kṛta duścikīrṣāṃstad vacanaṃ] (Bhatti. I. 20)
when he heard his words; &c.

THE PAST PASSIVE PARTICIPLES.

§ 896. The past passive participle is very frequently used to
supply the place of a verb; sometimes in conjunction with the
auxiliary verbs @[as] and @[bhū]. The past passive participle agrees
adjectively with the object in gender, number and case, the
agent being put in the Instrumental, while the past active
participle is treated exactly like the verb in the past tense; @[tena
kāryaṃ kṛtaṃ] by him the work was done; @[tena bandhanāni chinnaṃ] by him
the bonds were cut; @[ādiṣṭāsmi devyā dhāriṇyā] I am commanded
by queen Dharini; @[sa kāryaṃ kṛtavān] he did the work; @[rāmaḥ daityān
hatavān] Rama killed the Rakshasas; @[tvayā māṃ na avadīrṇam] thou
didst never despise me, &c.

§ 897. In the case of past passive participles of intransitive
roots the agent is put in the Nominative case; @[tadā paruruditō rājā
rakṣasāṃ] then the king of demons wept; @[satyaṃ mayā uktaṃ] &c.

§ 898. The past passive participles are often used imperson-
ally, the agent being put in the Instrumental case; @[vyāptaṃ] or
@[prakāśitaṃ sūryeṇa] it is shone by the sun; @[jitaṃ dārakeṣu] victorious (all
powerful) is the affection for sons; @[prakāśitavān paṇḍityam] he
showed his learning; @[vyāptaṃ] or @[pramoditaṃ mayā], &c.

§ 899. The past passive participles of the roots @[vid], @[sthā]
and @[śī] and their synonyms are used in the sense of the Present
tense and are construed with the Genitive. (See § 857.)

For further particulars vide §§ 705—797.

THE FUTURE PARTICIPLES.

§ 903 The future participle denotes that a person or thing
is doing, or is about to do the action or to undergo the condi-
tion, expressed by the root; @[kariṣyan] going or about to do; @[kariṣya-
māṇa] about to do or what is about to be done.

§ 904. Besides showing simple futurity participle expresses
intention or purpose, @[anuyāsyan ṛṣitanayām] wishing to follow
the daughter of the sage; @[dāsyan] wishing to give, @[vanyān
gṛhītvā iva vinīyamānān] wishing, as it were, to tame the wild beasts.
