(c) @[anu] meaning 'towards' or 'alongside of,' 'lengthwise,' is also similarly compounded; @[anuvanam]—(@[vanasya samīpe agacchat]) 'the thunderbolt passed towards the forest'; @[anugaṅgam vārāṇasī]—'Vārāṇasī is alongside the Ganges' (@[gaṅgādeśasamīpadeśavāsitvāt pārāvatītīyā bhavatyarthaḥ] q. v.).

§ 275. *The words @[pāra] and @[madhya] optionally form Avyy. compounds with any nominal base, and are placed first, the final @[a] being changed to @[e] when thus compounded; e. g. @[pare-madhye-gaṅgāyāḥ]—from the middle of the Gangetic stream; optionally of course, the Gen. form; also @[gaṅgāpārāt], @[gaṅgāmadhyāt]. In this case the Ab. termination is irregularly retained. If the final noun has the sense of the Loc. the final vowel may be changed to @[e]; as @[pare-madhye-gaṅge]; cf. @[gāṅgeyam] Bhatti. I. 4.

§ 276. (a) †@[anu] may optionally be compounded with a noun, showing relation by blood or by some kind of learning, to form an Avyy. comp.; @[dve vā karoti anuśiṣyam niṣṭhām], @[vyākaraṇasya niṣṭhām]; e. g. @[niṣṭhām anuśiṣyam], 'Sans. Grammar of which the three sages,—Pāṇini, Kātyāyana and Patañjali, are the authors in succession.'

(b) ‡and with names of rivers, this comp. also shows an aggregate; @[saptānu gaṅgam], @[daśānu vanam].

§ 277. ‖Any noun may be compounded with the name of a river, into an Avyy. comp. to form a name; @[unmattā gaṅgam], 'a place where the Ganges is very boisterous.' So @[lohitām], &c.

§ 278. The indeclinables @[samayā], @[nikaṣā], @[ārāt], @[abhitaḥ], @[paritaḥ], @[paścāt], do not combine with any noun; @[samayā grāmam], @[nikaṣā grāmam] &c.
