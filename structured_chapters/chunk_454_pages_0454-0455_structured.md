§ 768. The gerund of @[piṣ] is used with @[curṇam], @[cūrṇam] and @[peṣam]; @[cūrṇa-peṣam pinaṣṭi] = @[cūrṇam pinaṣṭi] इत्यर्थः (Sid. Kau.); so @[cūrṇa-peṣam pinaṣṭi] 'he grinds to powder,' @[udaka-peṣam].

§ 769. (a) With the words @[samūlam], @[akṛtam] and @[jīvagrahāya] are used respectively the gerunds in @[am] of the roots @[han], @[kṛ] and @[grah] in a cognate sense; @[samūla-ghātam hanti] 'kills destroying the roots i. e. totally'; @[akṛta-kāram karoti] 'does what ought not to have been done;' @[jīva-grāham gṛhṇāti] 'captures him so as to preserve him i. e. alive.'

(b) The gerund of @[han] and @[piṣ] is used with words signifying instruments (of action); @[pāda-ghātam hanti] = @[pādena hanti] 'strikes (kicking) with foot,' @[udaka-peṣam pinaṣṭi] = @[udakena pinaṣṭi] 'grinds with (using) the water.'

(c) Similarly the gerund of @[vṛt] and @[grah] is used with @[hastam] and its synonyms, and that of @[iṣ] with @[svam]; @[hasta-vartam vartayati]; so @[kara-vartam], @[hastena śalikām karoti] इत्यर्थः। Sid. Kau.; @[hasta-graham gṛhṇāti]; similarly @[pāṇi-grāham], @[kara-grāham], &c.

§ 770. The gerund of @[bandh] is used with words denoting peculiar arrangements of stanzas, &c., @[cakra-bandham badhnāti], @[gomūtrikā-bandhaḥ], @[padma-bandham baddhaḥ], @[mañjarikā-bandham], @[aṭṭālikā-bandham], &c.

§ 771. With the words @[jīvitam] and @[puruṣam] used subjectively the gerunds of @[naś] and @[vah] are used; @[jīvita-nāśam naśyati] @[jīvitam eva naśyati] इत्यर्थः, @[puruṣam vahati] @[puruṣo vahati] इत्यर्थः।

(b) The gerunds of @[hū] and @[pūr] are used with @[ūrdhvam] similarly; @[ūrdhva-havam hūyate], @[ūrdhvam eva hutiḥ hūyate] इत्यर्थः। @[ūrdhva-pūram pūryate], @[ūrdhvam eva ghaṭādir udakādinā pūrṇo bhavati] इत्यर्थः। Sid. Kau.

(c) Sometimes the Gerund in @[am] is used with words denoting a standard of comparison; @[ghṛta-nidhānam nidhīyate jalam] 'the water was kept with as much care as ghee,' @[ajaka-nāśam naṣṭaḥ aśvaḥ aśva iva naṣṭa] इत्यर्थः।
