§ 246-247] BAHUVRIHI. 151

III. BAHUVRIHI OR THE ATTRIBUTIVE COMPOUNDS.

§ 246. * A Bahuvrihi compound consists of two or more nouns in apposition to each other the attributive member being placed first and denotes—or refers to something else than what is expressed by its members. It generally attributes that which is expressed by its second member, determined or modified by what is denoted by its first member, to something denoted by neither of the two; e. g., @[mahābāhuḥ] 'one whose arm is great;' @[pītāmbaraḥ] 'one whose garment is yellow.' When dissolved it must have the pronoun @[yad] in any one of the oblique cases: as @[mahān bāhuḥ yasya sa mahābāhuḥ naḷaḥ]; @[pītaṃ ambaraṃ yasya sa pītāmbaraḥ hariḥ]. A Bahuvrihi compound partakes of the nature of an adjective and assumes the gender of the substantive it qualifies.

Note—In English such compounds are by no means rare; cf. high-souled, good-natured, narrow-minded &c.

N. B. The difference between a Karm. and Bah. comp. is this:—In the former, one of the member alone is in apposition to the substantive it qualifies, while in the latter the whole compound is an adjective. In the former the sense is complete in the comp. itself; in the latter it is not so; e. g. @[caturyāmaḥ nalaḥ], here one of the members viz. @[yāmaḥ] is in apposition to @[nalaḥ] and therefore the comp. is a Karm. @[gambhīranādaḥ] as a Karm. simply means @[gambhīro nādaḥ asti] 'a deep sound,' and the sense is complete; as a Bah. it is equivalent to @[gambhīro nādaḥ yasya] "some one whose sound is deep"; here the sense is not complete unless it is known whose sound is meant.

§ 247. Bahuvrihi compounds are divided into two classes, @[samānādhikaraṇabahuvrihi] and @[vyadhikaraṇabahuvrihi]. †
