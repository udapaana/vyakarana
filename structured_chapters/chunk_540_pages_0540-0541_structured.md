(d) Sometimes it is used to connect a subordinate fact with a main one @[bhikṣārtham aṭa mām cānaya] wander for alms and bring a cow; @[kuṭṭinī ca śāsitā gopī ca niḥsāritā kandarpaketuś ca dṛḍhīkṛtaḥ] the procuress was chastised, the cowherdess was expelled and Kandarpaketu was honoured.

(e) When the particle is repeated it has sometimes the sense of 'on the one hand,' 'on the other hand,' 'and' @[kva ca khalu gaganataṭasthitād atilolam ca ca niśitanipātāḥ aśarās te] where, on the one hand, is the extremely frail life of fawns, and where, on the other, are thy arrows hard like adamant and falling sharply; @[na gaṇyā sakalendumukhī ca sā śaṭham ced manmatha bikheśilam] on the one hand the full moon-faced lady is not easy to obtain and yet there is this unaccountable sport of love.

(f) Sometimes the repetition of @[ca] shows the simultaneous or undelayed occurrence of two events; @[ca ca prāpur udanvantaṃ buddhaś cādipuruṣaḥ] they reached the ocean and at the same time the primeval Being also awoke.

§ 967. @[tathā] 'likewise' often supplies the place of @[ca]: @[rāmas tathā lakṣmaṇaś ca] Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa; @[anāgatavidhātā ca pratyutpannamatis tathā] both Anāgatavidhātā and Pratyutpannamati; @[tathā hi] means 'for instance, to be more plain;' @[tathā ca] 'likewise,' both are often used introducing quotations.

§ 968. @[tu] but, @[hi] for, because, and @[api] are also excluded from the first place in a sentence. @[ātmā hi putraḥ sakhā bhāryā tu duḥkham] the son is one's own self, the wife one's friend, but the daughter a source of anxiety. @[apy ājñayā śāsita rātmanā na prāpto'si saṃbhāvayituṃ vanān mām | kālo hi saṃkramituṃ tvayā sahāpakārakṣamam āśramaṃ te]

§ 969. @[yadi] and @[cet] 'if' are usually used with the Potential or the Conditional; as @[yadi so'tra saṃnihito bhavet tadā mama sāhāyyaṃ kuryāt] if he were here he would assist me; @[yadi devadatto bhaviṣyat tad etad akariṣyat] had Devadatta been here, he would have undoubtedly done this; but they are also construed with the Present Indicative; @[yadi jīvati śreyaḥ paśyati] if he lives he will see prosperity; @[yadi mayā deśa pādānāṃ prayojanam asti] if Your Majesty has any thing to do with me, &c.; @[nijñāpayāmi mama jīvitena yadi vācā kathayasi] I conjure you by my life if you will not tell it in words. @[cet] is never used at the beginning of a sentence; @[yadi cet sahasrakiraṇo dhuri na praṇayati] (Śak. VII. 4) if the thousand-rayed one (sun) did not place him at the yoke of his car; @[api roṣaṃ kurṣva no cet] &c.. Bhā. P. I. 44.

The Particles @[atha] and @[khalu].

§ 970. @[atha] is used in the following senses:—(1) as a sign of auspiciousness*; @[athāto brahmajijñāsā] now begins the inquiry about Brahma; cf. the Bhāṣya on this sūtra. (2) marks the beginning or commencement of a work; @[atheyam ārabhyate prathamam tantram] now is begun the 1st Tantra; so @[atha śrīrāmāyaṇam], &c.; (3) 'then' 'after that,' &c.; @[atha prajanāmaś ceṣṭaḥ] &c. (Ragh. II. 1.) After that (i.e. passing of the night) the lord of the earth, &c. (4) asks a question @[atha bhagavān kaśyapo lokānugrahāya kuśalī kāryeṣu] is the venerable Kaśyapa all right that he may oblige the world? @[atha kiṃ śaknomi] are you able to eat? (5) 'and, including'; @[bhīmaḥ athaiva arjunaḥ] Bhīma and also Arjuna; (6) 'if;' @[atha avaśyam bhāvi jantoḥ] if death is sure to befall a creature, &c.
