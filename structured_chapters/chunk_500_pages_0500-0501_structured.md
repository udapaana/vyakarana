§ 842. * When a word expressive of 'a debt' is merely intended to be stated as a cause, it is put in the Ablative case; @[śatād grastaṃ dravyam] a thing mortgaged for a hundred rupees; @[ṛṇāt iva] @[baddhaḥ] tied down as it were by the debt he owes.

§ 843. (a) The Ablative often denotes the cause of an action or condition and may be translated by 'on account of, by reason of,' &c.; @[maunān mūrkhaḥ gaṇyate] one is considered a fool by reason of his silence (if he keeps silence); @[goghānād droṇāc ca] @[duritam] (H.) sin on account of my killing cows and men.

(b) The Ablative is used to make a causative assertion or to advance an argument; @[parvato vahnimān dhūmāt] | The mountain is fiery (has fire on it), because there is smoke; @[smṛtyanavaakāśadoṣaprasaṅgaḥ iti cen na anyasmṛtyanavakāśadoṣaprasaṅgāt] (Ved. Su. II. 1. 1) A disputant says—if you say that our argument is liable to the fault of giving no scope to your @[smṛti] then we reply: this your argument will not hold; for in that case other @[smṛtis] will have no scope given to them.

(c) The Ablative is used with comparatives or words having a comparative sense; @[bhaktimārgāt jñānamārgaḥ śreyān] the way of Knowledge is more efficient than that of devotion, @[aṇor aṇīyān] smaller than an atom; @[sahasramedhasahasrāt satyam eva śreṣṭham ucyate] truth alone is superior to a thousand horse-sacrifices, @[caitrarathād anūne] not inferior to Chaitraratha.
