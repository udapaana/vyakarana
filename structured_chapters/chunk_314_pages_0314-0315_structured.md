308 Sanskrit GRAMMAR. [§ 498-502

§ 498. Roots ending in @[ay] take @[o] instead of @[au] in the 1st and 3rd per. sing. and drop their final @[ā] before vowel weak terminations, and before such @[ā]s take the augment @[i]; as @[dadau], @[dadāva], @[dadima] 1st Per. @[dadātha], @[daditha] 2. Per. sing. of @[dā].

§ 499. Guna is substituted for the final vowel of roots ending in @[a] preceded by a conjunct consonant, and in long @[ṝ] and of the roots @[pṝ], @[vṝ] and @[vṛj]; as @[sasmarva] 1 dual of @[smṛ], &c.

@[hu] and @[ṛ] optionally take the Guna substitute before the weak terminations, and shorten their vowel when they do not; as @[juhuva], @[juhuvitha]; 1 dual of @[hu], &c.

§ 500. Monosyllabic roots, having a short @[a] for their penultimate, change it to @[e] and drop the reduplicative syllable before the weak terminations and before @[u] when it takes @[ū], provided their initial letter undergoes no change in the reduplication, e. g. @[pat] 1st dual @[papat] + @[va] = @[pet] + @[va] = @[petiva], @[petitha] 2 sing, @[seniva], @[senātha] 2. sing. of @[san], but @[nananditha] 2nd dual of @[nand] as the @[a] here is prosodially long.

Exceptions:—Roots beginning with @[a] and the roots @[gam] and @[han].

§ 501. @[ī] is inserted after the reduplicative syllable in the case of roots beginning with @[a] and ending in a conjunct consonant and in that of @[vyac] 'to pervade' and @[vraj] 'to go.' The reduplicative @[a] is changed to @[ā]: @[añj]—@[aañj] + @[ī] = @[ā] + @[n] + @[añj] + @[a] @[ānañj] + @[a] = @[ānaṅja]; similarly @[ānaṅja] 1 sing. of @[añj], @[ārcur] of @[arc], &c.

§ 502. The change of a semi-vowel to its corresponding vowel is called Samprasāraṇa. Samprasāraṇa generally takes place before weak terminations in the case of the following roots—@[vac], @[vad], @[vap], @[vah], @[vas] 'to dwell,' @[svap], @[yaj], @[vraj], @[grah], @[vyac], @[vyadh], @[pracc], @[ah] and @[vyādh]. In the case of the Perfect the roots @[svap], @[vraśc] and @[praśc] form an exception.
