PREPOSITIONAL COMPOUNDS.

4. @[Prādi].

§ 232. Those Tatpurusha compounds whose first member is a preposition are termed @[Prādi] by Sanskrit grammarians; some of these prepositions combine with a following noun in the Nom., with a noun in the Acc. &c.; e.g. @[pragataḥ ācāryaḥ prācāryaḥ] 'an eminent teacher', @[saṃgataḥ adhvānaṃ samadhvaḥ] 'taken to a path-way' (vide Bhatti. II. 45). @[pravāta ātapaḥ pravātaḥ] 'strong or excessive wind,' &c.; @[atikrānto mālāṃ atimālaḥ] 'excelling a garland' (in fragrance or beauty), @[atikrānto rathaṃ vā enaṃ vā atirataḥ] 'a chariot-warrior who has no rival or equal'; so @[atimāna] 'exceeding measure', @[atisarva] &c. @[ākarṣitaḥ kokilayā avakokilaḥ] 'attracted by the cuckoo'; @[pariglānaḥ adhyayanāt paryādhyayanaḥ] 'tired of studying', @[niṣkrāntaḥ kauśāmbyāḥ niṣkauśāmbiḥ] 'gone out of Kauśambi;' so @[niḥśoka] &c. A compound with prepositions which govern cases (@[karmapravacanīya]) is forbidden, except @[prati].

5. @[Gati].

§ 233. Compounds of the following words with verbal indeclinables are called @[Gati].

(a) The particles @[ūrṇī], @[uśrī], @[vaṣaṭ], @[svāhā], @[svadhā], @[prāduḥ], @[ārāt] and @[alam] and the word @[kārikā] meaning an action; as @[ūrṇīkṛtya], @[uśrīkṛtya] 'having accepted; @[vaṣaṭkṛtya] 'having uttered the word Vashat;' @[prāduḥkṛtya], @[kārikākṛtya] 'having done the act.'

(b) Onomatopoetic words not followed by @[iti], as @[khadkṛtya]; but @[khād iti kṛtvā] (@[nirīhāvat]).

(c) The words @[sat] and @[asat] showing respect and disrespect, respectively, the prepositions @[alam] in the sense of 'adorning', @[puraḥ], @[antaḥ], @[ataḥ], @[kaṇe], @[manaḥ], @[astam], @[accha], and @[tiraḥ]; e.g. @[alaṃkṛtya] 'having adorned', but @[alam kṛtvā] 'doing enough' (@[vyāptimitvarthaḥ] Sid. Kau.); @[puraḥkṛtya] 'placing in the front'; @[antaḥkṛtya] (@[bahiṣkṛtya]); @[antarhitya] (@[madhye kṛtvā] Sid. Kau.); @[kaṇekṛtya], as @[kaṇekṛtya payaḥ pibati] 'he drinks milk to his heart's content'; @[acchodya] 'having gone up to and spoken' (@[abhimukhṃ gatvā uktvā ca ityarthaḥ] Sid. Kau.); @[tirobhūya], @[manohitya] (till disgust is produced); @[astaṃgatya], @[acchagatya] 'going towards'.

(d) The words @[haste], @[pāṇau] and @[prāduhyam]; as @[hastekṛtya], @[pāṇau kṛtya], 'having married'; @[prāduhyaṃ kṛtya] 'having forced to yield'.

(e) The particles @[upāṃśu], @[anvāṃśu], @[sākṣāt], @[mithyā], @[amā], @[prāduḥ], @[ārāt] and @[namaḥ]; the words @[bhanasi], @[urasi], (except when there is the sense of juxtaposition or extreme contact); @[madhye], and the word @[alam] form compounds with verbal indeclinables optionally; @[upāṃśukṛtya], @[upāṃśu kṛtvā], @[anvāṃśukṛtya] @[anvāṃśu kṛtvā] 'giving strength to the weak' [@[durbalasya balam ādhāya ityarthaḥ] Sid. Kau.]; @[sākṣātkṛtya-tvā], @[lavaṇaṃkṛtya-tvā], @[urasikṛtya-tvā] (having accepted) @[bhanasikṛtya-tvā]; but @[urasi kṛtvā pāṇiṃ dadāti]; @[madhyekṛtya], @[madhye kṛtvā], @[pṛṣṭhe kṛtvā], &c.

(f) These compounds are also formed with nouns derived from roots for their second members; e.g. @[astamanam] 'setting of the sun', @[puraḥsāraḥ] 'reception, showing respect', @[tiraskāraḥ], @[alaṃkāraḥ], @[alaṃkṛtiḥ], &c.

§ 234. The @[lupta] formations (see Chap. XI.) are also classed as prepositional Tatpurusha compounds; as @[apanītatvam] having turned white what was not so before.
