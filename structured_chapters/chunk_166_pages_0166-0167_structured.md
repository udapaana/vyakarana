§ 259. * Two nouns alike in form in the Loc. and meaning 'something that can be seized,' or in the Instrumental, meaning 'weapons or things used as weapons,' may be compounded in a Bah. when the sense is 'the fight thus began' and the idea of an exchange of action is to be implied. In such compounds the final vowel of the first member is lengthened and that of the second is changed to @[i]. The compounds so formed are of the nature of @[Avyayībhāvas] and are indeclinable. The final @[a] takes @[Guṇa] substitute before the final @[i]. e. g. केशेषु केशेषु प्रवृत्तं युद्धं @[keśākeśi] 'a battle in which the warriors fight seizing each other's hair'; दण्डेन दण्डेन प्रवृत्तं युद्धं @[daṇḍādaṇḍi]; similarly @[astrāstri], @[hastāhasti], @[bāhubāhvi], @[nakhānakhi], &c.; but if the instruments used by both the parties be not the same, no compound is possible; @[gadāśūlī] will, therefore, be wrong.

Obs.—( a ) The following words also change their final to @[i]—दण्डो यस्मिन्प्रहरणे तद्‌ @[didaṇḍi]; similarly @[didṛṣṭi], उभा-उभया-@[cakṣu], @[ubhāhasti], @[ubhāhasti], उभा-उभया-@[pāṇi], @[ubhāpāṇi] &c.

§ 260. † The following @[Bahuvrīhis] are laid down as irregular. शोभनं प्रातरस्य @[supratara] 'one having an auspicious morning or a day having an auspicious dawn'; see Bhatti, II. 49. शोभनं श्वः अस्य @[suśva] 'having an auspicious morrow;' शोभनं दिवसं येन @[sudiva] 'one who has passed a happy day', @[śarī] कुक्षिरस्य @[śarīkukṣa] 'having a round belly;' चतुरस्र 'four-cornered, quadrangular,' एण्या ( of a female black deer ) इव पादौ अस्य @[eṇīpada]. @[ajapada], वृषभस्य इव पादो अस्य @[vṛṣapada] having the feet like those of a bull'.

§ 261. * The words @[uras], @[ghrā], @[upānah], @[dāri], @[pumān], @[śālīli] and @[artha] take क added to them when used in the singular, have a @[ka] added to them, when they form the latter member of a Bah. comp. सुन्दरम्‌ ( well-developed ) उरः यस्य स @[nyagdoraska], @[ghrtaghrska] 'one fund of ghee', &c. एकः पुमान्‌ यस्य असौ @[ekapumska] 'having one man only', &c.; when @[pumān] and the words following it are used in the dual and the plural they take @[ka] optionally, @[dvipumska] or @[dvipumsa] &c.

( a ) @[artha] takes the final @[ka], necessarily when preceded by @[an] and optionally in any other case; @[anarthaka] 'useless'; but @[apārthaka] 'meaningless' &c.

§ 262. † A fem. Bah. ending in @[a] takes this @[ka] necessarily; as बहुदण्डिका नगरी 'a city having many ascetics'; बहुवाग्मिका सभा 'an assembly having many eloquent speakers in it'; but बहुदण्डिकः or बहुदण्डिको ग्रामः ( vide § 263 below ), as it is mas.

§ 263. ‡ And as a general rule, when the final word of a Bah. comp. does not undergo any additions or alterations mentioned in the foregoing rules, it optionally takes the affix @[ka]. @[ugraśasodarśanaḥ]-@[ugraśaṅkaḥ] 'of mighty glory'; but @[uttarapūrva], @[vyāghrapāda], @[mahodaraḥ] &c.

§ 264. But if the last word of a Bah. compound be a fem. noun ending in @[ī] or @[ū] not capable of being changed into @[in] or @[un] before the vowel case termination, or if it ends in @[ī], @[ka] is necessarily affixed; बहुः कर्ता येषां तत्‌ @[bahukṛtyakaṃ jagat], @[bahunadīko deśaḥ], बहती वधूः यस्य स @[bahuvadhukaḥ], &c.; but @[prājñaḥ brāhmaṇaḥ] is an exception; @[bahu-kaḥ], @[bahu-raḥ] &c.
