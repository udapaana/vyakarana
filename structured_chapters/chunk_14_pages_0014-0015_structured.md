The word @[kāra] is therefore used as an affix to denote a particular letter; e.g. @[jakāra] the letter @[ja], @[ṣakāra] the letter @[ṣa], &c.

§ 11. A vowel by itself or a consonant, simple or conjunct, with a vowel added to it, is called @[akṣara] or a syllable.

§ 12. The forms which the vowel signs assume when added to consonants and the changes which some letters undergo when compounded are given below, under (a) and (b).

(a) @[a] added to a consonant is denoted by the removal of the nether stroke; as @[k+a=ka]. The remaining vowels when compounded with a consonant become @[ā, i, ī, u, ū, ṛ, ṝ, e, ai, o, au], respectively; as @[k+ā=kā], @[k+i=ki]; similarly @[kī, ku, kū, kṛ, kṝ, ke, kai, ko, kau].

Exception:—@[ṛ] when following upon @[ra] remains unchanged, as @[rṛ].

(b) In compounding consonants they should be taken in the order in which they are pronounced; the last consonant takes a vowel, the preceding ones generally losing their nether and perpendicular strokes when combined e.g. @[tana] ought to be written as @[tna], @[gna] as @[ṇṇa], &c. Some letters, however, change their form slightly and others entirely, when compounded with other consonants; e.g. @[lpa; tra; cca; gra], &c.; @[ra] immediately preceding another consonant (or the vowel @[ṛ]) is denoted by the sign ~ written above the following consonant as @[rka]. It is then necessarily called @[repha].
