Note. 1.—The difference between the two is this:—In the former the common quality of comparison is actually expressed; while in the latter it is left to be understood; in fact if it be expressed there can be no compound; as @[puruṣaḥ vyāghra iva dhṛṣṭaḥ].

Note. 2.—The above Karmadhārayas may be dissolved as @[mukhaṃ candraḥ mukham], @[mukhaṃ kamalaṃ mukhakamalam], &c. Dissolved in either way there will be no difference in the formation of the compounds; but there will be difference both in the meaning and in the metaphor. In one case prominence will be given to @[candra] or the moon and the figure will be '@[upamā] (a simile)'; while in the other to @[mukha] or the face in which case the figure will be '@[rūpaka]'. Compounds like @[pādaṃ padmaṃ pādapadmam], @[vidyā eva dhanaṃ vidyādhanaṃ], &c. are termed @[avadhāraṇāpūrvapadakarmadhāraya].

§ 222. An adjective may be compounded with the noun it qualifies in most cases† as @[nīlaṃ ca utpalaṃ ca nīlotpalam], a blue lotus &c.; @[kṛṣṇaśvaḥ sarpśca kṛṣṇasarpaḥ]; where the comp. is necessary; but @[śyāmadantasya śyāmadantaḥ]. Such compounds are called @[viśeṣaṇapūrvapadakarmadhāraya].

As a general rule in such compounds the qualifying word is placed first; but there are several exceptions which are given below.

* So when the common attribute is applicable in its expressed (@[abhidha]) sense to the @[upameya] as in @[prasanna sauhasya] dissolve the comp. as @[mukhaṃ padmam iva] and when to the @[upamāna] as in @[paṅkajam vikasitam] dissolve as @[mukham iva padmam].

† @[viśeṣaṇaṃ viśeṣyeṇa bahulam]. Pān. II. 1. 57.

(a) *The qualified word is placed first and is always of the mas. gender if it denotes a class or species, when compounded with certain adjectives; e.g. @[ibhayuvatiḥ] 'a young female elephant', @[agniśabalaka] 'a small fire', @[udaktikatipayam] 'butter-milk mixed with a little water', @[gopyūṣaḥ] 'a cow that has brought forth a calf for the first time', @[gojemuh] 'a milch-cow (@[navaprasūtikā])'; @[gobandhyā] 'a barren cow', @[govedhat] (@[garbhaghātinī goḥ]), @[govaṭsatarī] 'a cow whose calf is one year old', @[kāṭhagnihotraḥ] 'an agnihotrin Brāhmaṇa of the Kaṭha branch of the Yajurveda', @[kāṭhādhyāpakaḥ] 'a teacher of the Kaṭha branch', † @[go-mataḍikā-mavarikā-prakāṇḍa] (also mas. according to some) -@[uṣṭha-vṛṣa] 'an excellent cow'. The last mentioned five words, all of which denote excellence, retain their own gender invariably; as @[brāhmaṇamataḍikā] 'an eminent Brāhmaṇa' &c.; but @[kumārī mataḍikā], as no class is meant here.

(b) ‡The words @[kaḍāra], @[khañja], @[khoḍa] lame, @[kāṇa], @[kuṇḍa] blunt, dull, @[khalati] bald-headed, @[gaura], @[indha], @[kubja] fan, @[piṅgala], @[pāṃḍu], @[śuṣka] hard, stiff, @[śveta], @[lohita] and @[hrasva], may optionally precede the words in apposition to them, as @[jaiminikaḍāraḥ] or @[kaḍārajaiminiḥ] Jaimini who is turned tawny on account of his having practised penance in the sun, &c.
