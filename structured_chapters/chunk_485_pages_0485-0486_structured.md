(b) The prepositions @[abhi], @[anu], @[pari] and @[prati] are used with the Accusative when they refer to particular things; गिरिम् अभि-अनु-परि-प्रति-विद्योतते विद्युत् the lightning flashes towards the mountain; also when they mean 'to every one,' &c. वृक्षम् अभि-अनु-परि-प्रति-सिञ्चति waters each and every tree; so अभि-अनु-परि-प्रति वृक्षं वृक्षं जातम् अन्नथः।

(c) @[anu], @[pari] and @[prati] also govern the Accusative when they imply 'one's own share,' यस्मात् अनु-परि-प्रति लक्ष्मीः Lakshmi is the proper share (property) of Hari.

§ 806. The roots given in the following Karika govern a double Accusative—

दुह्याच्छप्रच्छिचिब्रूशासजिमन्थस्तेनयश्च याम् ।
कर्मद्वय युज्यादकर्मकाणि च तथा स्याज्जीहृकृष्वहाः ॥

i. e. the roots @[duh] to milk, @[pac] to cook, @[daṇḍ] to punish, @[rudh] to obstruct, @[prach] to ask, @[ci] to collect, @[brū] to speak, @[śās] to instruct, @[ji] to win (as a wager), @[manth] to churn, and @[stey] to steal; as also @[nī], @[hṛ], and @[kṛṣ] and @[vah]; and the synonyms of these; गां दोग्धि पयः he milks (draws milk from) the cow; बलेः याचते पृथ्वीम् he begs the earth of Bali; तण्डुलान् ओदनं पचति he cooks rice (into food); similarly दर्गान् शतं दण्डयति, बलस्य वरुणद्धि गाम्, माणवकं पन्थानं पृच्छति, वृक्षम् अवचिनोति फलानि, माणवकं धर्मं ब्रूते-शास्ति, अक्षान् जयति देवदत्तम्, दधि अमृतं मथ्नाति, देवदत्तं शतं स्तेनाति, चामम् अजां नयति-हरति-कर्षति-वहति वा; So माणवकं धर्मं भाषते-वक्ति वा, बलेः पृथ्वीं भिक्षते; &c. शत्रून् अगमयत् स्वर्गं वेदान् अवेदयत् स्वान् ।

That venerable Hari is my refuge who despatched the enemies (of gods) to the next world, explained the meaning of the Vedas to his (followers), made the gods drink nectar, taught Veda to the Creator and seated the earth (made it rest) on water.
