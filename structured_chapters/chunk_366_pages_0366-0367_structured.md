460 SANSKRIT GRAMMAR { § 590-592

§ 590. There are three kinds of Passive Verbs:—(1) the Passive properly so called (@[karmaṇi] qo), as @[rāmeṇa granthō dīyate]; (2) an Impersonal Passive (@[bhāve] qo), as @[gamyate] 'it is gone,' and (3) a Reflexive (@[karmakarttari] qo), as @[odanaḥ pacyate] 'rice is cooked.'

CONJUGATIONAL TENSES.

§ 591. Formation of base:—

(a) @[ya] is added to the root, which is weak i.e. no Guṇa or Vṛddhi substitute takes place before it; @[nī-nīya], @[bhid-bhidya].

(b) Before @[ya] roots undergo the same changes as they do before the Par. terminations of the Benedictive; @[ji-jīya], @[ci-cīya], @[sṛ-sriya], @[ṛ-riya], @[kṛ-kriya], @[pṛ-pūrya], @[bandh-badhya], (@[vid-vidya], @[nind-nindya]), @[vac-ucya], @[vah-uhya], &c.

(c) The final @[ā] (original or substituted) of @[dā] 'to give,' @[dhe], @[do], @[mā], @[sthā], @[pā], @[hā] 'to drink,' @[so], @[hā] 'to abandon,' is changed to @[ī]; in other cases it remains unchanged; @[dā] and @[do-dīya], @[dhe-dhīya], @[hā-hīya]; but @[khyā] 'to cut, to purify'—@[khyāya], @[jñā-jñāya], @[trā-trāya].

§ 592. The passive base is conjugated like a root of the 4th class in the Atm.; e.g.

"It might even be suspected, that the occasional assumption of an Intransitive signification and a Parasmaipada inflexion by a passive verb, was the cause which gave rise to a 4th class of Primitive verbs as distinct from the Passive. Instances are certainly found of passive verbs taking Parasmaipada terminations, and some passive verbs (e.g. @[jāyate], 'he is born,' fr. @[jan]; @[pūryate], 'he is filled,' fr. @[pṛ]; and @[tapyate] 'he is heated,' fr. @[tap]) are regarded by native grammarians as Ātmane. verbs of cl. 4. Again, many roots appear in class 4 as Intransitive verbs, which also appear in some one of the other nine as Transitive. For example, @[yuj], 'to join' when used in a Transitive sense, is conjugated either in cl. 7, or in the Causal; when in an Intransitive, in cl. 4. So also @[puṣh], 'to nourish,' @[kṣhubh], 'to agitate;' @[kleś], 'to vex'; @[sidh], 'to accomplish.'"

Monier Williams.
