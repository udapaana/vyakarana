486 SANSKRIT GRAMMAR. { § 819-822

@[Sampradāna] ( संप्रदानम्‌ ). The indirect object of the root @[dā] is called @[Sampradāna]; also the person or thing with reference to whom or which an action is done; @[vipra gāṃ dadāti] 'he gives a cow to a Brahman,' @[yuddhāya saṃnahyate] 'prepares for battle,' @[na śūdrāya dharmaṃ brūyāt] 'The Śūdra should not be instructed in the Veda, &c.'

But in the case of the root @[yaj] the proper object is put in the Instrumental case and the indirect object in the Accusative, @[rudrāya vṛṣaṃ yajate] 'he sacrifices a bull to Rudra,'

Note:—Though the root @[dā] ought to govern the Dat. of the indirect object, it is sometimes found used with the Gen. or the Loc, @[rājyaṃ siddhārthaṃ te vai dadāmi tava khecara] 'O sky-wanderer, I will give you the prosperous kingdom of the Śibi;' @[yastvāṃ rāme pṛthivīṃ dātumicchasi] 'thou who art thinking of bestowing the earth upon Rāma,' &c.

§ 820. † The roots @[ruc] and others having the same sense govern the Dative of the person or thing pleased or satisfied; @[haraye bhaktiḥ rocate] 'Hari likes devotion; @[apāṃ hi tṛptāya na vāridhārā svāduḥ śītalā sagandhā svadante tuṣārā] 'a stream of sweet water, cool and perfumed, is not liked by one who has already drunk enough of water.'

§ 821. ‡ The roots @[ślāgh] 'to praise,' @[hū] 'to hide,' @[sthā] 'to stand,' and @[śap] 'to swear,' govern the Dative of the person to whom some object or feeling is to be conveyed, @[gopī smarādṛṣṭā kṛṣṇāya ślāghate-hūte-tiṣṭhate-śāpate vā] 'a cowherdess being incited by love flatters Kṛṣṇa or apparently conceals her feeling from him or waits for him or swears before him; ( Vide Bhatti. VII. 73, 74. ); but @[rājānaṃ ślāghate mantrī] 'a minister praises his king.'

§ 823. * The roots @[kruṣ], @[duh], @[druhyati], and @[asūy], and others having the same sense govern the Dative of the person against whom the feeling of anger, hatred, &c, is directed; @[hṛṣaye kṛṣṇo druhyati-īrṣyati-asūyati vā] (Sid. (a) † But @[krudh] and @[druh] preceded by a preposition govern the Accusative case; @[kiṃ māṃ saṅkrudhyasi] 'why do you get angry with me?' @[nityamasmaccharīramābhidrogdhuṃ yatate] (Mud. I.) 'he always endeavours to do harm to my body.'
