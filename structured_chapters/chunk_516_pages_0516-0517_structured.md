COMPARATIVE AND SUPERLATIVE DEGREES.

§ 887. Adjectives in the Comparative degree are used with the Ablative; @[अधर्मात् श्रेयः क्षत्रं प्रतिपालनम्] protection (of one's subjects) is better than aggrandizement. @[जङ्घुनाद्युधिष्ठिरो ज्यायान्] Yudhiṣṭhira was older than Arjuna.

(2) Sometimes the comparative is used with the Instrumental, @[प्राणैः प्रियतरः] dearer than life. See also § 863 Note.

§ 888. The superlative may either be construed with the Genitive or Locative; @[अयमेतेषां एतेषु वा गरिष्ठः गुरुतमो वा].

§ 889. The sense of the comparative and the superlative may also be expressed by the particular case used; @[अस्य हृदयं पाषाणादपि कठिनम्] his heart is harder than stone; @[छात्राणां मध्ये वा छात्रेषु पण्डितः] Chaitra is the cleverest of all students.

§ 890. The words @[वर] and @[प्रवर] when used in the sense of the superlative govern the Genitive or the Locative, @[पुत्रः स्पर्शवतां वरः] 'a son is the best of things possessed of touch,' @[चतुष्पदां गौः परा लोहानां काञ्चनं वरम्] 'the cow is the best of quadrupeds and gold of metals'; the neut. sing of @[वर] is used (with a word expressive of negation) in the sense of 'better and not, or but not;' @[अकरणमन्यकरणं वरम्] doing any thing slowly is better than not doing

Section IV.
PARTICIPLES.

§ 891. All declinable participles in Sanskrit partake of the nature of adjectives i.e., they agree with the nouns they qualify in gender, number, and case. The participles often discharge the functions of verbs. They are largely employed to take the place of the Past and Future tenses and more especially of passive verbs. When so employed they follow the same rules of syntax as are laid down for the roots from which they are derived.

PRESENT PARTICIPLES.

§ 892. The present participle is to be used when contemporaneity of action is to be indicated. It is often idiomatically used to express the sense of 'while' or 'whilst' in English; @[अरण्ये] while wandering in the forest; @[मङ्गलसूत्रं बिभ्रत एवं] while Repeat wore the marriage string.

Vide 670. (3).

§ 893. * The present participle is used to denote the manner in which an action is done or the cause or object of an action; @[शयाना भुञ्जते यवनाः] the Yavanas dine by lying down; @[हरिं दृष्ट्वा मुच्यते] a man is absolved by (reason of his) seeing Hari; similarly @[तिष्ठन् मूत्रयति, मण्डन अवकषति] (Mb.).

§ 894. The roots @[आस्] and @[स्था] are generally used with present participles to show the continuity of the action denoted by them; @[प्राणिनां हन्तुं भास्ते] used to kill (always kept on killing) animals; @[तं प्रतिपाढयन् तस्थौ] remained waiting for him.
