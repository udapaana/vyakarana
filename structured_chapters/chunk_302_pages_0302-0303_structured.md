§ The following couplet gives the endings of these roots and their number in each:—

@[ka ca cha ja da dha na pa ya va śa ṣa sa ha smṛtāḥ kram] ।
@[ekonaikaika catasraḥ ṣaṇ mātraḥ pañca ṣoḍaśa smṛtāḥ] ॥

The first line gives the ending consonants and thus enables the students to know at once which root is Set and which is Anit; e. g. @[gam] may be at once known to be a Set root as @[ma] does not occur in the first line. The second line gives the number of roots ending in a particular consonant. Thus roots ending in @[ka] are @[eka] i. e. one (@[ka] being the first consonant) in number, roots ending in @[ca] are @[catasraḥ] i. e. four (@[ca] being the 6th consonant); roots ending in @[cha] are @[eka] i. e. one, in number, and so on.
