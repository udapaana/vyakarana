§ 363. The indeclinables comprise (I) Prepositions (II) Adverbs, (III) Particles, (IV) Conjunctions, and (V) Interjections.

Besides these there are in Sanskrit a few nouns having one inflection only which are treated as indeclinable (@[nipāta]s); e.g. @[anyat] another (reason), @[ratam] setting, @[asti] that exists, @[om] the well-known sacred syllable om, @[manas] satisfaction, food, @[svadhā] coaxing, @[namas] a bow, @[nāsti] non-existence, @[kṣā] the earth, @[dyaus] the sky, @[jyāti] the dark fortnight, @[śrī] happiness, @[śukla] or @[śuci] the bright fortnight, @[saṃvat] a year, @[havis] food offered to gods, @[svadhā] food offered to the manes, @[svar] heaven, @[svasti] happiness &c.

I. PREPOSITIONS.

§ 365. A preposition, styled '@[Upasarga] or @[Gati],' in Sanskrit is an indeclinable word, having an independent meaning and prefixed to verbs and also to their derivatives. These prepositions modify, intensify and sometimes totally alter the senses of roots†:

* @[na tiṅāḥ śluśca sarvāsu vṛttiṣu liṅ vigrahe ca | sarvanāmasthānam āvasyantat śeṣe apy avyayam] | Pāṇ. I. 1. 37.

† @[bhāsvaraṃ vākyaṃ karma karoti vibhaktiḥ | dravyam eva viśeṣayan upasargas gatis tadā ||]

Cf. also Sid. Kau. @[upasargeṇa dhātvartho balād anyatra nīyate | prahārāhārasaṃhāravihāraparihāravat ||]

Some think that prepositions have no meaning of their own, but they simply bring to light when prefixed to roots their hidden senses. (cf. Bh. 15).

§ 366. The following are the prepositions (@[upasarga]s) commonly prefixed to roots:—

@[ati]—'beyond, over', @[atikrama] 'overstepping' or 'going beyond', @[atidāna] 'a gift, &c.'

@[adhi]—'over, above, &c.'; @[adhigama] 'coming up, acquisition,' @[adhikāra] 'high office or power,' @[adhikṣepa] 'censure,' &c.

@[anu]—'after, behind, along, &c.' @[anukramaṇa] 'following,' @[anukāra] 'imitation,' @[anugraha] 'favour,' &c.

@[apa]—'away from, away,' @[apanayana] 'the act of taking away,' @[apahṛ] 'to plunder, seize' &c., @[apakarṣa] 'harm, wrong' &c.

@[api] (sometimes @[pi])—'near to,' 'over,' 'taking to,' &c.; @[apihṛ] 'to be resolved into or reduced to,' @[apidhāna] or @[pidhāna] 'a covering,' @[apaya] 'destruction,' &c.

This prep. is more commonly used as an independent adverb having a cumulative force in classical Sanskrit.

@[abhi]—'towards, near to,' &c; @[abhigam] 'to go towards,' @[abhijana] 'noble descent or family,' @[abhimāna] 'self-respect,' @[abhijñ] 'to defeat,' &c.

@[ava]—(sometimes @[va] see @[api] and @[va], note) 'away, down,' &c. @[ava] or @[vagāhṛ] 'to plunge into,' @[avaroha] 'descent,' @[avagīta] 'reproached,' @[avaman] 'to disrespect,' &c.
