§ 187. When the several members of a Dwandwa compound
are viewed separately, it is called Itaretara Dwandwa, e. g.,
@[dhavakhadirau chindhi], 'cut down the Dhava and the Khadira trees'
In this species each member is of equal importance i. e. has its
own independent meaning. It takes the dual or the plural
according as the objects denoted by it are two or more in num-
ber; the gender of the final noun is the gender of the whole;* as
@[kukkuṭaś ca] a cock @[mayūrī ca] a pea-hen @[kukkuṭamayūryau imau] (fem. dual of
@[mayūrī]); but @[mayūrī ca kukkuṭaś ca mayūrīkukkuṭau imau]; @[rāmaś ca]
@[lakṣmaṇaś ca rāmalakṣmaṇau], @[rāmaḥ lakṣmaṇaḥ bharataḥ śatrughnaḥ] &c.

Exception—@[aśvaś ca kharaś ca aśvakharau] ( mas. dual) a horse and
a mare; @[ahaś ca rātriś ca ahorātraḥ] ( mas. sing. day and night ).

§ 188. @[Samāhāra] Dwandwa is that species of Dwandwa
which implies an aggregate of the things enumerated in which
constitute a complex idea. It is always singular and neuter.
Thus @[āhāra-nidrā-bhaya-maithunāni] does not merely mean 'food, sleep and fear'
but all that characterises animal life. In this species the
meaning of the different members is subordinate to the
collective sense of the whole compound.
