§ 916. The root @[अर्ह्] 'to deserve' when used (in the second person) in combination with the infinitive expresses 'a request, a respectful entreaty,' and is generally equivalent to the English 'I pray, be pleased, &c.;' @[अग्निं शामयितुम् अर्हसि] (Megh. 56) please put out the fire; @[न चेद् रहस्यं प्रतिवक्तुम् अर्हसि] (Kum. I. 40) if you have nothing to conceal from me, please answer me; @[द्विजात्यहान्य् अवधारयितुम् अर्हसि लोमेव] (Ragh. V. 26) pray wait for two or three days, O respectable one! &c. Sometimes it is equivalent to a gentle command; @[तां प्रसादयितुम् अर्हसि] (Ragh. I. 89) you ought to please her, @[न तं शोचितुम् अर्हसि] you ought not to bewail him. In the third person and under the same circumstances it expresses power or ability and can be translated by 'can;' @[द्रोणं हि संयुगे कोऽन्यः सोढुम् अर्हति पाण्डवान्] Mah. Bhar. IV. 58. 27. @[दैवे पुत्रे अप्रबोधने कः शोचितुम् अर्हति] Ibid I. 1. 246.

§ 919. Besides the general sense given above the Present tense in Sanskrit has the following senses:—

(a) † It is sometimes used in the sense of 'immediate futurity,' @[कदा गमिष्यामि] when wilt thou go? @[एषः गच्छामि] Here I go (i. e. shall &c.); @[ऊर्ध्वम् अहम् इतो म्रियामि] I shall die an hour after.

(b) It may also be used to denote an action which is recently completed, @[कदा त्वं नगरात् आगतोऽसि]—@[अहम् आगच्छामि] when didst thou come from the city? Here I come (have come just now).

(c) In narrations it is used for the Past tense, @[गृध्रः ब्रूते कोऽसि] the vulture says, 'who art thou'?

(d) Sometimes it is used to denote a habitual or repeated action; @[पशुघ्नेन असौ जीवति].
