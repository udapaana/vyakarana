476 SANSKRIT GRAMMAR. [ § 797-800

@[harim] @[sevate] he worships Hari; @[antam] @[gacchan] @[tṛṇam] @[spṛśati] going to a village he touches grass.

§ 798. All transitive verbs govern an Accusative; @[puṣpāṇy avacินoti] collects flowers; @[apaḥ sasarjādau] ( the creator ) created water first, &c. Several transitive verbs govern what is called in English a factitive object besides a direct object; @[tvām āmananti tvām eva prakṛtiṃ tvām eva puruṣaṃ viduḥ] Kum. II. 13. they consider thee to be Prakriti, they know thee to be Purusha; @[kumāram netāram kṛtvā] having made Kumara the leader ( of the forces ); @[putram tam ātmasaṃmānam ajanayat] he made his son Aja by name.

§ 799. Intransitive roots govern the Accusative nouns denoting space or country, or duration of time and distance, @[kurūn svapiti] he sleeps in the country of the Kurus; @[tatra katipayān divasān atiṣṭhat] there he dwelt for some days, @[godoham āste] he sits down till a cow is milked; @[krośam prayāti] he walks for a Kosa, @[krośam kuṭilā nadī] the course of the river is winding for a Kosa; but @[māsasya dvir adhīte] studies twice a month; @[krośasyaikadeśo parvataḥ] the hill is situated in a part of a Kosa.

§ 800. Roots having the sense of motion, real or metaphorical govern the Accusative of the place to which it is directed. @[grāmam gacchati] goes to a village; @[adhijyadhanvā vicacāra dāvam] with his bow strung he roamed all over the forest, @[ānandasya parām koṭim anvagacchan] they reached the highest point of joy; @[manasā kṛṣṇam eti] goes to Krishna (thinks of him) mentally; @[evam cintayan dhyāne nidrām anvagat] while thus pondering he sank into sleep.

(a) When the motion is real, the Dative may also be used; @[grāmāya yāma vā gacchati]; but not of words denoting 'a road;'

* But when the relation of object and verb is expressed by the passive termination the noun forming the object is put in the nominative case, @[hariḥ sevyate]

† @[kālādhvanoratyantasaṃyoge] Pān. 2. 3. 5.

§ 801. The roots* @[śī], @[sthā] and @[ās], with @[adhi], govern the Accusative of the place where the action takes place; @[adhiśete adhitiṣṭhati adhyāste ca vaikuṇṭham hariḥ]; @[śilāpaṭam adhiśāyī] reclining on a stone-slab; @[indrasiṃhāsanam ardhabhāgam adyāsthau] occupied half of Indra's seat; @[adhyāsta sarvartukṣemām ayodhyām] lived in Ayodhyā delightful in all seasons.

§ 802. † @[vid] with @[abhini] governs the Accusative; @[abhiniviśate dharmamārgam] he pursues the path of goodness; @[dhanyā sā veśyākumārikā yām eva bhavan mano'bhiniviśate] happy is that harlot girl on whom you have fixed your mind ( see Bhaṭṭi. VIII. 80. ). Rarely this governs the Loc. @[abhiniviśate pāpe] fondly resorts to sin. @[viś] with a preposition governs the Acc. but with @[upa] meaning 'to sit' takes the Loc. @[āsane'smin upaviśa] sit on this seat.

§ 803. The root @[vas] preceded by the prepositions @[upa], @[anu], @[adhi] and @[ā] governs the Accusative case; @[upa-anu-adhi-ā-vasati ca hariḥ] Hari dwells in a place; @[anyonya-nyam anvavasad raṇam] he dwelt in a dreary forest; but @[vas] with @[upa] meaning 'to abstain from food' is used with the Loc., @[upavasan vane rāmaḥ] Rama observes a fast in the forest.
