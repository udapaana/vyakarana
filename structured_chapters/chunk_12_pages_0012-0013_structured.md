§ 4. The consonants are divided into @[sparśa] or mutes (those involving a complete closure or contact and not an approximate one of the organs of pronunciation), @[antaḥstha] (or intermediate, the Semivowels), and @[ūṣman] or sibilants.

These are represented by thirty-three syllabic signs arranged as below:—

(1) @[kavarga] or the group @[ka]—@[ka] k, @[kha] kh, @[ga] g, @[gha] gh, @[ṅa] ṅ.

(2) @[cavarga] or the group @[ca]—@[ca] c, @[cha] ch, @[ja] j, @[jha] jh, @[ña] ñ.

(a) mutes (3) @[ṭavarga] or the group @[ṭa]—@[ṭa] ṭ, @[ṭha] ṭh, @[ḍa] ḍ, @[ḍha] ḍh, @[ṇa] ṇ.

(4) @[tavarga] or the group @[ta]—@[ta] t, @[tha] th, @[da] d, @[dha] dh, @[na] n.

(5) @[pavarga] or the group @[pa]—@[pa] p, @[pha] ph, @[ba] b, @[bha] bh, @[ma] m.

§ 5-6. THE ALPHABET. 5

These are also called the five classes designated as @[kavarga], @[cavarga], @[ṭavarga], @[tavarga], and @[pavarga] respectively.

(6) Sibilants—@[śa] ś, @[ṣa] ṣ, @[sa] s.

(7) Semivowels—@[ya] y, @[ra] r, @[la] l, @[va] v.

(8) Sonant Aspirate—@[ha] h.

Besides these we have two more characters occurring in the Veda, viz. @[ḷa] and @[ḷha] (often substituted for @[ḍa] and @[ḍha]; as @[aḷam] for @[aḍam], @[tiḷhyati] for @[tiḍhyati] &c.). In Marāṭhī @[ka] is generally substituted for the final @[a] of Sanskrit words.

§ 5. The first two letters of the five classes and the sibilants are called surds or hard consonants. The rest are called sonants or soft consonants.

§ 6. In addition to the characters given above there are in Sanskrit two nasal sounds:—the one called @[anusvāra], is denoted by @[ṃ], i.e. a dot placed above the letter after which it is to be pronounced, e.g. @[kaṃ]; the other, called @[anunāsika] is denoted by @[m̐], i.e. a dot within a semi-circle placed above the letter after which it is to be pronounced, e.g. @[kam̐].

(a) And a sort of hard breathing is known as @[visarga] (generally called @[visarjanīya] by Sanskrit grammarians). It is denoted by the sign: @[ḥ], i.e. two vertical dots placed after the letter after which it is to be pronounced. In pronunciation it is a harder aspirate than @[ha]. The @[visarga] is not an original character but only a substitute for a final @[r], @[s] or @[ḥ].

(b) @[jihvāmūlīya] and @[upadhmānīya] are terms given to a sort of Semi-Visarga, when pronounced before @[ka] or @[kha], and @[pa] or @[pha] respectively. It is written symbolically +.
