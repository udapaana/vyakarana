The Sanskrit infinitive, on the other hand, can never be made the subject of a verb, admits of no Accusative before it, and can only express indeterminate time and incomplete action. Wherever it occurs, it must be considered as the object, and never the subject of some verb expressed or understood. As the object of the verb, it may be regarded as equivalent to a verbal substantive, in which the force of two cases, an Accusative and Dative, is inherent and which differs from other substantives in its power of governing a case. Its use as a substantive, with the force of the Accusative case, corresponds to our use of the Latin infinitive; thus @[तत्‌ सर्वं श्रोतुम्‌ इच्छामि] 'I desire to hear all that,' 'id audire capio,' where @[श्रोतुम्‌] and audire are both equivalent to Accusative. Similarly, @[रोदितुम्‌ आरब्धा] 'she began to weep,' and @[महीं जेतुम्‌ आरभे], he began to conquer the earth, where @[महीजयम्‌ आरभे], he began the conquest of the earth, would be equally correct."

§ 913. The infinitive is used with verbs and verbal nouns meaning 'to wish or desire' provided the agent of it is the same as that of the verb; @[को जिगीषति हरेः हनुं द्रष्टुम्‌] ( Mud. § 914. The infinitive is also used with:—

(a) verbs meaning to be able, to make bold, to know, to be wearied, to strive, to begin, to set about, to bear, to be pleased and to be; @[न शक्नोति शिरोधरां धारयितुम्‌] ( Kad.) is not able to support his neck; @[जानासि कोपं निग्रहीतुम्‌] you know how to restrain anger; @[अङ्गदेन समं योद्धुम्‌ अचेष्टत] (Bhatti. XV. 77) he strove to fight with Angada; @[गन्तुं व्यवस्येद्‌ भवान्‌] (Meg. 22.) thou wilt try to go; @[वक्तुम्‌ आरम्भयस्व] (Meg. 103.) begin to speak; @[भक्ष्यं भोक्तुं जिघृक्षते वा] (Sid. Kau. ) desires to eat food, &c.

(b) words like @[अर्ह] and others meaning sufficient or able, proficient or skilled in, &c.; @[अर्हसि प्रजाः पालयितुम्‌] ( Rag. X. 25 ) thou art able to protect the creation; @[कः दैवम्‌ अन्यथा कर्तुम्‌ अर्हति] who is able to change destiny; @[स्थातुम्‌ अर्हन्ति त्वया तुलनां प्रासादाः] ( Meg. 66 ) the palaces are able to stand comparison with thee, @[भोक्तुं शीलवान्‌ भोजः पटुः] (Sid. Kau. ) skilled in eating.

(c) words having the sense of 'it is time to do anything;' @[कालः समयो वेला अथवा बा भोक्तुम्‌] (Sid. Kau.) it is time to take food.
