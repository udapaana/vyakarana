**§ 785.** The predicate may not always be a finite verb; but a participle, or an adjective or a noun may take its place.

(*a*) When a participle is used as the predicate it must agree with the subject in number and gender; @[sa tad uktavān] he said that, @[sā tad uktavatī] she said that, @[teṣāṃ bandhanāni chinnāni] their bonds were cut off, @[kāryaṃ kṛtam] the work is done, @[latā chitā] the creeper is cut; &c.

(*b*) When an adjective or a noun is used as the predicate, a form of the roots @[bhū] or @[as] may be used with it or may be omitted; the adjective used predicatively agrees with the subject in number and gender; nouns like @[āśraya], @[pātra], @[bhājana], @[sthāna], @[pada] &c. retain their gender and number; @[subhṛtyaḥ durlabhaḥ] a good servant is difficult to be obtained, @[susutaḥ pituḥ harṣapada] a good son is the object of his father's pride, @[sampadaḥ padamāpadām] riches are the abode of miseries, @[sa tu tasyā abhiprāyabhūmiḥ] &c. in these cases the verb agrees with the subject in number and not with the noun used predicatively; @[sampadaḥ āpadāṃ padāni santi] and not @[asti], &c.

**§ 786.** When a substantive or an adjective is used predicatively with verbs of incomplete predication such as 'to grow, to seem, to be, to appear,' &c. the substantive or adjective so used must agree with the subject in case; @[iyam me niścitaḥ] this is my resolve; @[sa mahārājaḥ kṛśaḥ jāgarayā bhāti] that king seems emaciated through wakefulness; @[abhavat trailokyeśaḥ] desirous of being the lord of the three worlds.

(*a*) The same rule applies to transitive roots of incomplete predication used passively; @[tena munināṃ sa mūṣako biḍālaḥ kṛtaḥ] by that sage the mouse was made (transformed into) a cat, &c.: @[rājā hi viṣṇuḥ manyate] a king is thought to be Vishnu.

**§ 787-789** SYNTAX. 473

**§ 787.** When an indeclinable used with an adjectival force takes the place of the verb, the object governed by it is put in the Nominative case. @[viṣavṛkṣo'pi saṃvardhya svayaṃ cchettum asāmpratam] (Ram. II. 55.) it is not fit to cut down even a poisonous tree having first reared it up; here the indeclinable @[asāmpratam] is equivalent to @[na yujyate] and the whole sentence to @[pūrvaṃ saṃvardhya taṃ vṛkṣam asāmpratam (na yujyate) yo'pi viṣavṛkṣaḥ syāt].

**Concord of the Adjective with the Substantive.**

**§ 788.** An adjective, participial or qualitative, must agree with the substantive it qualifies in gender, number and case; @[rūpavān puruṣaḥ] a handsome man; @[rūpavatī nārī] a beautiful woman; @[mahat saṅkaṭam] a great calamity, @[ete puruṣāḥ], @[tāni bālakāni]; @[gacchanī nārī], &c.

But numeral adjectives of fixed gender and number remain unchanged:—@[śataṃ brāhmaṇāḥ] a hundred Brahmanas, @[śataṃ striyaḥ] a hundred women, @[viṃśatiḥ bālakāni] twenty children.

**§ 789.** When an adjective qualifies two or more substantives it agrees with them in their combined number; when the substantives differ in gender, the adjective will be masculine when the substantives are masculine and feminine, and neuter when they are masculine, feminine and neuter @[rājā rājñī ca stutyacaritau staḥ] the king and his queen are of laudable conduct; @[dharmaḥ kāmaśca dapaśca kīrtiḥ krodhaḥ sukhaṃ vayaḥ arthādetāni sarvāṇi vardhante na saṃśayaḥ] fulfilment of duty, satisfaction of desires, pride, anger, happiness and long life; all these proceed undoubtedly from wealth.

(*a*) Sometimes the adjective takes the gender of the majority of the substantives; @[vṛddhau ca mātāpitaro bhāryā cāpyātmano priyā | putraśca dārakaścaiva bharitavyā munā navīt | evaṃ hi manavā gītam] aged parents, a good wife and a young son should be maintained even by doing a hundred foul deeds; so has Manu spoken (laid down).
