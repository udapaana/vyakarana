§ 217. Additional instances of the @[aluk] of the Loc.

(a) @[aluk] is necessary in the case of the words—(1) @[nabhas] and @[yudh], followed by @[stha]; as @[nabhaḥsthiraḥ], "stationary in the sky, @[yudhisthiraḥ] firm in battle; (2) @[hṛd] and @[div] followed by @[spṛś], @[hṛdispṛk], @[divispṛk] (@[hṛdayaṃ divaṃ ca spṛśatīti]); (3) @[anta] and @[madhya] followed by @[śara], @[madhyeśaraḥ], @[anteśaraḥ] and (4) words expressive of parts of the body except @[mūrdhan] and @[mastaka] and not followed by @[kāma]; @[kaṇṭhekālaḥ], @[uraḥśmasruḥ] (having the hair on the breast: Bah.), but @[śiraḥmastakaśikhaḥ], @[yasya kāmo'sya akṣakāmaḥ].

(b) When a noun in the Loc. is compounded with a verbal derivative and the whole is a name, @[aluk] takes place in most cases; e.g. @[stamberamaḥ] (@[stambe] a kind of grass @[ramate asau]) an elephant; @[karṇejapaḥ] one who whispers into the ear, a spy; @[khecaraḥ] a heavenly being; @[jalesayaḥ] a lotus; @[kuśeśayaḥ], @[jaleśayaḥ] a fish; but @[kuśāśayaḥ], @[sarojaḥ] or @[sarojam] &c.

(c) There is optional @[aluk] of the Loc. of a word expressive of time when compounded with @[kṛta] and words ending in the affixes @[ka], @[tama] and @[tara]; @[pūrvāhṇakṛtaḥ] or @[pūrvāhṇe-kāre]; @[ahnastama] or @[ahnaḥtamaḥ] &c. @[ahnastara] or @[ahnaḥtaraḥ].

(d) When the compound is not a name, @[aluk] is necessary when @[ja] comes after @[vāsanta], @[śārada], @[kāla] and @[diva]; and optional when it comes after @[hemanta], @[grīṣma], @[śarat] and @[varṣa]; as @[vāsantaśāradakāladivijḥ]; @[graiṣmakaḥ] or @[grīṣmajaḥ] produced in the rainy season, &c.

Exceptions:—No @[aluk] takes place in the case of verbal derivatives ending in @[in] and the words @[śayi], @[baddha], @[siddha] and @[sthita] in the ordinary language, as @[sthaṇḍileśāyī] 'an ascetic,' @[sākalyasiddhaḥ], @[cakrebaddhaḥ]; @[sameṣṭhaḥ]; but @[chatraṃ'syākṣareṣṭhaḥ] &c.
