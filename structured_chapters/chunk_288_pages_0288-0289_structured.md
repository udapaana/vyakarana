288 SANSKRIT GRAMMAR [§ 444-449

Note—The first syllable of a reduplicated root is called the reduplicative syllable; e.g. the first @[ci] in @[cicet], or first @[ju] in @[juhoti].

§ 445. If a conjunct consonant begins a root, the first consonant only with the following vowel is reduplicated; e.g. @[grabh-jagrabh].

(a) If the first member of a conjunct consonant, however, be a sibilant (@[s], @[ṣ]) and the second a hard consonant, the hard consonant is reduplicated: @[smi-sismi], @[ṣṭhu-taṣṭhu]; but @[svan-sasvan] &c.

§ 446. A radical aspirate (2nd or 4th letter of a class) is changed to its corresponding unaspirate in the reduplicative syllable; as @[dadh], @[dagh-dadagh], @[bhas-babhas], &c.

§ 447. The guttural of the reduplicative syllable is changed to the corresponding palatal (subject to the above rule) and @[h] to @[j]; e.g. @[kam-kakam-cakam], @[khan-khakhan-cakhan], @[hu-juhu], &c.

§ 448. A radical long vowel becomes short and @[ṛ] is changed to @[a] in the reduplicative syllable; as @[dā-dadā], @[nī-ninī], @[kṛ-cakṛ] &c.

§ 449. The penultimate @[e] or @[ai] and @[o] or @[au] become @[i] and @[u] respectively in the reduplicative syllable: @[sev-sisev], @[doh-dudoh], &c.
