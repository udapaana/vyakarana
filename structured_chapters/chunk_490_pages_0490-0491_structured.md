484 SANSKRIT GRAMMAR. [ § 816-817

§ 815. A characteristic attribute indicative of the existence of a particular state or condition is put in the Instrumental; @[jaṭābhiḥ tāpasaḥ] he is an ascetic (which is apparent) from his matted hair (@[jaṭājñāpyatāpasatvaviśiṣṭa ityarthaḥ] Sid. Kau.).

§ 816. The Instrumental is also used to express the cause or motive or the object of purpose of an action as distinguished from the mere instrument of it; @[puṇyena dṛṣṭo hariḥ] Hari was seen by (virtue of) merit; @[tenāparādhena daṇḍyosi] thou art to be punished for that fault; @[adhyayanena vasati] dwells for the purpose of studying; also when the object to be accomplished is simply implied; @[alaṃ yatanena] away with your efforts i.e. they will not succeed; @[yatnamantareṇa sādhyaṃ nāsti ityarthaḥ] (Sid. Kau.)

§ 817 The Instrumental is used with words expressing the idea of—:

(a) excelling; @[pūrvaiḥ mahābhāga bhavatyatiśete] Oh fortunate one, you excel your ancestors in that (devotion); @[atiśete ravidīdhitimapi dhāmnā sahasradhā caṇḍaḥ] (Mu. 3.17.) he surpasses, by his lustre, the lustre of the sun, @[vīryatā khalu bhīmena vanalate bhīmatāṃ gatāḥ] | Sak. I.

(b) resemblance, likeness, equality; @[svareṇa pitaram anukurute] resembles his father in voice; @[dehābandhena svareṇa ca rāmābhyāṃ udāratvamiti] (Utra. 4.); @[asya vaktraṃ mātuḥ mukhena saṃvadati] his face resembles that of his mother; @[vikrameṇa tulyaḥ viṣṇoḥ] equal to Viṣṇu in valour.

(c) swearing, @[bharatenātmanā cāhaṃ śape] I swear by Bharata and myself, @[śāpitāsi mama jīvitena] I conjure thee by my life.

(d) rejoicing, being pleased, @[bhaktyā gurukulampadayā ca prītāsmi] I am pleased with thee by thy devotion to your preceptor and compassion upon me; @[kāpuruṣaḥ svalpakenāpi tuṣyati] a low person is satisfied with little.

(e) motion (the conveyance or the part of the body on which a thing is carried being put in the Instrumental case): @[rathena gacchati] 'he moves about in a chariot.'

(f) price (real or metaphorical) at which a thing is bought; @[śatena krītaḥ] bought for a hundred (rupees, etc.); @[svaprāṇavyayenāpi rakṣaṇīyāḥ sahāyāḥ] 'the life of a friend ought to be saved even at the cost of one's life.'

§ 818. The following words also govern the Instrumental case:—

(a) Words expressing the idea of use or need, such as @[kim], @[kāryam], @[arthaḥ], @[prayojanam] and the like, and the root @[vyā] with @[ḍī] used in the same sense; @[dhanena kim yasya] &c. what is the use of his wealth who &c.; @[tṛṇena kāryaṃ avatiṃsvarāṇām]—'even the rich sometimes stand in need of grass,' similarly @[ko'rthaḥ putreṇa jātena yo na vidvān na dhārmikaḥ], @[na svāmipādānāṃ mayā sthitiprayojanam], &c.

(b) The particles @[alam] and @[kṛtam] meaning 'enough;' @[alaṃ ruditena] 'enough of your weeping (do not weep);' @[kṛtaṃ atiprasaṅgena] 'away with overpressing'; @[alam] is also used with a gerund as @[alaṃ anyathā saṃbhāvya] 'away with misunderstanding.'

(c) Particles expressing 'accompaniment' such as @[sākam], @[ārdham], @[sārddham], @[saha], &c.; @[tvayā sākaṃ mayā gantavyam] (Bh. VIII. 70.); @[yuvaṃ bhavyā sārddhamasi prapannaḥ] Rag. XIV. 63: @[dṛḍharo niveśyatāṃ samaṃ hariṇāṃganābhiḥ] | Sak I. 27. &c.

(d) Words meaning 'having or destitute of,' @[saṃpannopyarthaḥ parimāvapādaṃ yāti kṛpaṇaḥ] 'though possessed of wealth,' &c.; @[arthena hīnaḥ] 'destitute of wealth.'

N. B. For the optional use of the Instrumental see the Abl., the Gen. and the Loc. cases.
