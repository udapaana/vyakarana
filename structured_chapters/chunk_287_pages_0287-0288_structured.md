Rules of Reduplication:—

§ 444. The first vowel of a root together with the initial consonant, if any, is reduplicated i. e. doubled, as पठ् = पप्ठ्, उज्झ् = उउज्झ्, &c. after reduplication.

282 SANSKRIT GRAMMAR. [§ 444-449

Note—The first syllable of a reduplicated root is called 'the reduplicative syllable'; e. g. the first प in पप्ठ्, or first उ in उउज्झ्.

§ 445. If a conjunct consonant begins a root, the first consonant only with the following vowel is reduplicated; e. g. प्रथ् = पप्रथ्.

(c) If the first member of a conjunct consonant, however, be a sibilant (श, ष, स) and the second a hard consonant, the hard consonant is reduplicated: स्तु = तस्तु, स्फुट् = पस्फुट्; but स्वन् = सस्वन्, &c.

§ 446. A radical aspirate (2nd or 4th letter of a class) is changed to its corresponding unaspirate in the reduplicative syllable; as भू = बभू, ध्या = दध्या, घुष् = जघुष्, &c.

§ 447. The guttural of the reduplicative syllable is changed to the corresponding palatal (subject to the above rule) and ह to ज; e. g. कम् = ककम् = चकम्, खन् = खखन् = कखन् = चखन्, हृ = हजृ, &c.

§ 448. A radical long vowel becomes short and ऋ is changed to अ in the reduplicative syllable; as भू = बुभू, नी = निनी, कृ = चकृ, &c.

§ 449. The penultimate ए or ऐ and ओ or औ become इ and उ respectively in the reduplicative syllable: सेव् = सिसेव्, दोह् = दुदोह्, &c.
