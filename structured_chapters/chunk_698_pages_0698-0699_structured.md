§ 1–5] APPENDIX.
PROSODY.

§ 1. Poetical composition in Sanskrit may be in the form of '@[gadya]' prose, or '@[padya]' verse or metrical composition.

§ 2. Prosody treats of the laws of versification or metrical composition. Sanskrit verse is regulated by quantity, not by accent.

§ 3. A @[śloka] or stanza consists of four lines each called a @[pāda] or quarter. A @[pāda] is regulated either by the number of syllables (@[akṣara]) or by the number of syllabic instants (@[mātrā]).

(a) A syllable is as much of a word as can be uttered distinctly by one effort of the voice; i. e. a single vowel with or without one or more consonants.

(b) A '@[mātrā]' (@[mātrā]) is the measure of time required to pronounce a short vowel.

§ 4. A syllable is @[laghu] 'light,' or @[guru] 'heavy' according as its vowel is short or long.

(a) The vowels @[a], @[i], @[u], @[ṛ] and @[ḷ] are short and the vowels @[ā], @[ī], @[ū], @[ṝ], @[e], @[ai], @[o], and @[au] are long. When a short vowel is followed by an @[Anusvāra] or @[Visarga] or by a conjunct consonant, it is converted into what is called 'a prosodially long vowel;' as @[gandha], @[accha], &c.
