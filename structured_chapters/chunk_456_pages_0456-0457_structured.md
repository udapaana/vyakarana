§ 772. Nouns having the sense of the Ablative and the Accusative are used with this gerund of a root when haste or hurry is intended; @[शय्योत्थाय] @[व्रजति] 'runs having quickly got up from the bed;' @[अश्वोत्थाय] @[युध्यन्ते]; @[शिबिकोत्थाय], &c.

§ 773. *A root may be used in its gerundive form in @[त्वा] with its object prefixed to it if that object be a part of the body which can be severed without fatal effects; @[भ्रूभङ्गं] @[कथयति] 'narrates contracting the eyebrows;' but @[शिरश्छित्त्वा] @[वदति] (not @[शिरश्छेदम्]) as the severance of the head would cause death.

(b) Similarly when a part of the body is completely hurt or pressed in the action, the gerund in @[त्वा] is used with that part; @[उरःप्रतिपेषं] @[युध्यन्ते] i.e. @[उरःपीडयन्तः] 'afflicting the whole bosom,' @[उरोविदारं] @[प्रति] @[खस्करे] @[नखे]।
