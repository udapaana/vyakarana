446 SANSKRIT GRAMMAR §§ 761-763

(5) THE ADVERBIAL INDECLINABLE PARTICIPLE
OR
The Gerund in @[am]

§ 761. There is another Indeclinable Participle having the same sense as that in @[tvā], formed with the affix @[am]. Before this the root or the derivative verbal base generally undergoes the same changes as it does before the @[i] of the Pass. Aorist 3rd sing.; @[nī-nāyam] 'having carried or led'; @[dā-dāyam] 'having given', @[sthā-sthāyam], @[bhid-bhedam], @[grah-grāham], @[gam-gamam], &c.

§ 762. This Participle is generally used at the end of compounds; @[sa loṣṭaghātam hataḥ] 'he was pelted to death;' @[bandim kṛtvā] (Vik. I.) 'She was taken a captive, @[sa śatrūn samūlaghātam abhijaghāna] (Bhatti. I. 2.) 'He totally exterminated his enemies, &c.'

§ 763. The participles in @[tvā] and @[am]* when repeated yield the sense of repetition of their action or condition expressed by the verb; @[smṛtvā smṛtvā] or @[smāram smāram],—'having repeatedly remembered'; @[pītvā pītvā] or @[pāyam pāyam] 'having drunk again and again,' similarly @[bhuj-bhuktvā bhuktvā] or @[bhojam bhojam]; @[śru-śrutvā śrutvā] or @[śrāvam śrāvam]. @[gam-gantvā gantvā] or @[gamam gamam] or @[gāmam gāmam], @[labh-labdhvā labdhvā] or @[lambham lambham] or @[lābham lābham], @[pralam-pralambham pralambham], @[jāgar-jāgaram jāgaram],† &c.

* @[ābhīkṣṇye ṇal] | Pan. III. 4. 22.

† At the end of compounds this may not be repeated and yet have the sense of repetition; as in:
@[kākutsthaḥ kusumagrāmaṃ gṛhṇan nayanavīkṣayan kupadam |
sarito vāri piban yatra ramyaphalake ramanta āsa] || Bhatti. VII. 11.
'The descendant of @[Kakutstha], smiling gently, repeatedly bending down the creepers would pluck their flowers, wading through every stream (that he came across), would sip the waters, and seating himself on every charming slab would remain there (in admiration of the scenery).'

§ 764. The Gerund @[am] does not, in many instances, express the notion of repetition.

§ 765. This gerund or the one in @[tvā] is used with the words @[adya], @[prathamam] and @[purā], used as prepositions, although the notion of repetition is not present in these compounds, @[adyabhojam bhaktvā vā vrajati] 'having first eaten he goes out.' So @[adyabhojam] or @[prathamamabhojam] or @[purābhojam] or @[purā] @[vrajati].

§ 766. The gerund in @[am] of the root @[kṛ] is used*

(a) with the noun governed by it, if it be compounded with it prepositionally when censure is implied, @[caurakāram ākrośati] (@[cauraśabdam ucāratyarthaḥ]) 'cries out (he is) a thief, a thief.' In this case @[ṇvul] is added to the noun governed.

(b) with the words @[svādu], @[lavaṇa], and @[sampanna], @[ṇvul] being added to these as before; @[asvādukam svādukāram kṛtvā śakte] @[svādulavaṇasampannakāram bhuṅkte] 'he eats having sweetened or seasoned his food.'

(c) with the words @[anyathā], @[evam], @[ittham], and @[katham], provided the root @[kṛ] loses its sense; @[anyathākāram vadati] 'he speaks in a different manner;' @[evamīkāram bhuṅkte] 'he eats thus:' similarly @[itthamkāram], @[kathamkāram], but @[śironyathā] @[evam] @[ittham].

(d) with the particles @[yathā] and @[tathā] when an angry reply is given; @[yathākāram bhokṣye tathākāram mokṣye kim tavanena] (Sid. Kau.); 'I will eat in this manner, I will eat in that manner, what have you to do with that?'

§ 767. The Gerund of the roots @[dṛś] and @[vid]† is compounded with their objects and expresses totality; @[kanyādṛgjham vṛṇīte] 'chooses as many girls as he sees, i.e. all of them,' @[strīvittam]
