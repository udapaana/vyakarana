tive; यड्युयुनु रुदुरं यजुते 'he sacrifices a bull to Rudra.'

Note:—Though the root दा ought to govern the Dat of the
indirect object, it is sometimes found used with the Gen. or the
Loc. यस्य त्युत्युर्नों दुवुयुने वै वुदुरुङ्युयुट् सर्युकैचुरु 'Oh sky-wanderer, we
will give rain at the prosperous kingdom of the Śibis;' मुस्युवं रामं
मुधिविरी दाद्युयुरिकुच्छुटुति 'thou who art thinking of bestowing the earth
upon Rāma,' &c.

§ 820 † The roots रुच् and others having the same sense
govern the Dative of the person or thing pleased or satisfied;
हुरै रोचुते कृष्ण: Kṛṣṇa likes devotion; अस्मै न स्पृहुर् न सतिस्युयुहुः
स्युयुङुः इगुणियुः स्युदुते तुयुषुयुरु 'a stream of sweet water, cool and per-
fumed, is not liked by one who has already drunk enough of water.'

§ 821. ‡The roots स्तुयुयुष् 'to praise,' गुट् 'to hide,' स्था 'to stand,'
and मुयु 'to swear,' govern the Dative of the person to whom
some object or feeling is to be conveyed, गोर्नों स्युसुयुद् कृयुयुयुयु
श्युयुरुते-नूनं-कृष्णुं-स्तुरीयु 'a cowherd; else Kṛṣṇa instilled courage into
flatters 'Kṛṣṇa or apparently conceals her feeling from him
or waits for him or swears before him;' (Vide Bhatti. VII. 73.
74,); but रानानं स्तुयुयुते मन्त्रो 'a minister praises his king.'
