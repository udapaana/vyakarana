Note—The first syllable of a reduplicated root is called 'the reduplicative syllable; e. g. the first प in पपत्, or first उ in उउत्.

§ 445. If a conjunct consonant begins a root, the first consonant only with the following vowel is reduplicated; e. g. प्रच्छ्=पप्रच्छ्.

(:) If the first member of a conjunct consonant, however, be a sibilant (श, ष or स) and the second a hard consonant, the hard consonant is reduplicated. स्तुभ्=तुष्टुभ्; स्कन्द्=चस्कन्द्; but स्वन्=सुस्वन् &c.

§ 446. A radical aspirate (2nd or 4th letter of a class) is changed to its corresponding unaspirate in the reduplicative syllable; as चित्=चिचित्, धूह्=दुधूह्, भुज्=बुभुज्, &c.

§ 447. The guttural of the reduplicative syllable is changed to the corresponding palatal (subject to the above rule) and ह to ज; e. g. कृ=चकृ; गम्=जगम्; पवि=तिपवि; सक्त्=तसक्त्=चसक्त्, हृणु &c.

§ 448. A radical long vowel becomes short and ऋ is changed to अ in the reduplicative syllable; as पा=पपा, वी=विवी, धू=दुधू &c.

§ 449. The penultimate इ or उ and आ or ओ become इ and उ respectively in the reduplicative syllable; सेध्=तिसेध्, शोक=जुशोक, &c.
