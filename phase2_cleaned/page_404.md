Ātम. उत्तपते or विततपे सूर्यः when used transitively it is Ātम.
If it has a limb of the body of the agent for its object; उत्तपते
or विततपे पानिं 'he warms his hands. but सूर्यः सुवर्णांगरातपति: when it means
'to practise penance' it is Ātम. and is conjugated like a root
of the fourth class.

According to some तप् with अनु is Ātम; अनुतपते 'repents,'

दा—*'to give' (3 cl.) by itself takes either pada; but when-
preceded by आ it is Ātम. in any other sense than opening (the
mouth, &c.); पदं आदत्ते 'accepts money;' विद्यां आदत्ते 'acquires
knowledge;' नादते भवता रुद्ररेऽ या पहुट्य (Sāk.) 'does not pluck
your foliage through devotion;' but गात्रं आदानः स्यायाति 'opens
mouth;' द्वीत्वोकं सादेति शुदः: 'the doctor opens the tumour on
the root;' नदीद्वुरं स्यादद्वाति 'breaks open the bank of a river;'
but if the mouth belongs to another the exception is removed;
स्यादद्वते पत्रकुस्य मुखं पिपीलिकाः 'ants break open the mouth of a
moth' (Mah. Bhā.).

दा†—'to give' (I cl.) when preceded by the preposition सम्
simply or coupled with any other preposition takes the Ātम.
provided it is used with the Instrumental in the sense of the
Dative; दासरा सुनुस्यत्ते or संपदस्यत्ते 'gives (something) to the
maid-servant,' but दासरा धनं संपदस्यदति विद्यां 'gives wealth to a.
Brāhmaṇa through the maid-servant'

दृश्‡—'to see preceded by उप and used intransitively takes the
Ātम.; स्वर्गं चेति इति-क वेद्य; 'this root takes the Ātम. in
the Desiderative; दिदृक्षते 'wishes to see.'

हृ—'to run' is Par, in the Cau,

नह्—with सम् is Ātम. in the sense of 'preparing, being ready
