(a) When the nouns derived from the roots नह्, सद्, इष्, व्यध्, रुच्, सह, and तन् with the aff. क्विन् ( o ) follow prepositions termed Gati or nouns which they govern; उपनाह्, नीवृद् an inhabited country, a realm, वीवृद् the-rainy season, मर्माविद् piercing the vitals; so मृगविद् a hunter (cf. Bhaṭṭi II. 7). नीरुह्, अभीरुह्, शत्रूरुद् overpowering an assailant परीसह्. But परिधावनम् as नह् is not followed by Kvip.

(b) When followed by the aff. वल, and the whole is a name; कषीवलः a husbandman.

(c) Words containing more than two vowels when followed by वत् (मत्) except in the case of अजिर, रविर, क्षिणिन, इरिच, कारणवत् and चरणवाक and the whole is a name; अमरावती and इरावती which are names; but अजिरवती, कृदिमती, बहुमती which is not a name. Also in the case of हर, वंश, भूम, अति, कपि, तनि, शुचि, and इण; इरावती &c.

(d) When a verbal derivative with the aff. अ (घञ्) follows a preposition, in most cases, when the comp. does not signify a human being; परिधानम्:-परीपाकः; but निषादः one of a mountain-tribe. Similarly प्रति-सी-वंशः; प्रति-सी-कारः &c.

(e) When काश follows a preposition ending in इ; नीकाशः, नीकाशः; but प्रकाशः

(f) When अट्टन् is the first, and नर् the second member of a compound, and the whole is a name; अटानर: gold, अष्टानरः a spider; but अष्टउत्तरः, विद्यातरः an epithet of Savitṛi.

(g) When मित्र is the latter member and the whole is the name of a ṛishi; विश्वामित्रः; but विश्वामित्रो माणवकः ।
