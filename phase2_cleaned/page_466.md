(a) The singular may be used to denote a class; सिंहः
शार्दूलानाम् the lion is the king of beasts; मुक्तामणिरत्नेषु नरः श्रेष्ठः &c.

(b) The dual sometimes denotes the whole of the
same class; पितरौ parents; चाटकौ a male and a female sparrow.

(1) Note:—Words like द्वं, द्वितयं, द्वा, युमं, &c. meaning
'a pair,' &c. which are dual in sense but singular in form ought
to be always used in the singular, except when several pairs
are meant.

(2) Note:—Words like हस्तौ, नेत्रे, पादौ, &c. should always
be used in the dual in Sanskrit.

(c) The plural, like the singular, may represent a class,
ब्राह्मणाः पूज्याः (or ब्राह्मणः पूज्यः) Brāhmaṇas (i. e. the Brāhmaṇa
class) are adorable.

(1) The plural is not unfrequently used as a mark of res-
pect or reverence. भवति श्रीशङ्कराचार्याः so (says) the venerable
Śaṅkarāchārya; भूतं आचार्यगुणाः this is the opinion of the
reverend preceptor, &c.

(2) In the first person, great personages and writers some-
times use the plural instead of the singular; वयमपि भवन्तो
किमपि पृच्छामः we (i. e. I,) too ask you something; भूतं तु वयं
but we (i. e. I, the writer) hold this opinion; वयमपि च निरा-
क्षिमेः we rule over speech (i. e. language).

(3) Words like द्युतः, यज्ञाः, अश्वदाः, सिक्ताः, आयुः, गुणाः, द्राजाः,
&c. are always used in the plural, though some of them are
singular in sense.

(4) Names of countries which are really the names of the
people inhabiting them, must be used in the plural, स विदेहान्
उपगर्यः he went to Videha, &c.

But in the case of compounds ending in words denoting a
country, such as देश, विषय, &c. the sing. must be used; आसीत्
मगधेषु पाटलिपुत्रं नाम नगरं there is a town called Pāṭaliputra
in the country of Magadha.

(5) The plural of proper nouns denotes a family or race
as in English; जनकानां रघूणां च वंशस्तं गोत्रमङ्गतम् Utt.
