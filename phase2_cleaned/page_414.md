                       CHAPTER XIV.

                VERBAL DERIVATIVES
                          or
                 PRIMARY NOMINAL BASES.
           Derived by means of the Kṛt Affixes.

§ 665. The Kṛt affixes (Vide § 337) are added to roots or
to their modified forms to form nouns, adjectives, and indecli-
nables; e. g. कृ—कार, कर्वत्, कुर्वण, कुर्व, कारुस्पत्, चङुर्वर्, कृत्वा
कर्वुन् &c. These are called Kṛdantas or Primary Nominal Bases
as distinguished from the Secondary Derivatives formed with
the Taddhita affixes.

§ 666. There is a peculiar class of Kṛt or Primary affixes
technically designated by Sanskrit grammarians—Unādi or
those beginning with the affix उण् i. e. the affix उ with the
signs or indicatory letter न्; इष्व called from the two roots सिष्,
याङु, &c. in the first Sūtra being derived with the affix. These
Unādi affixes form primary nouns, like other Kṛt affixes form
verbal roots, but are classed separately because their applica-
tion is limited, because the nouns derived by their means are
either formed irregularly, or the connection between their
senses and the meanings of roots from which they are
supposed to be derived is not so clearly discernible as in the
case of other primary derivatives; e. g. अस्वुवर् अस्थान स्यातोतिति
or जस्यः 'a horse' which is derived either from आस् 'to pervade,'
or from अस्यच् 'road;' and आपु with वि, &c,. काड् 'an artisan'
from कृ 'to do,' &c.
