This is also added to roots when a root signifying a standard

sound like that of a camel;' श्वपकुङ्गयनि 'one who cries like a

be indicated; दर्भवायनि 'one who observes the vow of
sleeping on the altar,' also in the sense of 'necessity' or the
payment of a debt; अवदर्शनि 'what takes place of necessity,'
शतदानि 'one who pays off a debt of Rs. 100.'

इष्ण—(इष्णुच्, तिष्णुच्) is added to छृ with अध and तिरस्, वृत्
with नि, पच्, पद् and भूद् all with उप, रच्, वच् with अव, दृय,
शह्, and चत् in the sense of 'in the habit of, possessed of the
property of,' or 'expert in;' 'अधवासम् acting well,' 'तिरस्कृष्णम् decora-
ting, skilled in decorations; तिरस्कुहं छृत्तिहरयं तिरस्कृतिष्णुः (see
Bhatti. V. 1) 'repudiating, turning aside,' अपहनिष्णुः 'clever
in flying up,' वररत्, वरिष्णुः, सरिष्णुः, रोविष्णुः, &c.; it is found
added to some other roots also by poets; e. g. प्रभविष्णुः 'power-
ful,' मनिक्षष्णुः 'respected,' स्थयिष्णुः, &c.

इयस् and इवांस्—(निधःयस्, यच्छःवांस्) are added to यस् with the words
अयुस्, हयस्, मदुस्, वरस्, यशु, अय and निर in the active
sense of भावदर्शनम् 'being what a thing or person was not
before;' अयुहयस्, अयुश्शस् पूर्णोयति अयुस्यंभविष्णुयस् or—भावत् 'becom-
ing rich not being rich at first,' (see Bhatti. III. 1) so अयुस्यं,
करूणयस्, &c.

उ (इ and इत्)

उ—is added to Desiderative bases to form nouns; चिकीर्षु,
desirous of doing; विजिगीषु desirous of conquering, &c.; and
to आश् with आ, भिक्ष्, विद्, and इक्ष्; आशंसु: desirous, hopeful,
भिक्षु: a beggar; विद्वांसु: one who knows, &c.; इक्षु: इक्षुः waxing.
