§ 798. All transitive verbs govern an Accusative; पुष्पाण्युप-
चिनोति collects flowers; अप एव सर्वजन्त्युयुनो (the creator) created
water first, &c. Several transitive verbs govern what is called
in English a factitive object besides a direct object; प्राकृतान्:
प्रकृतिं त्युक्तो नं पुनरुङु विदुःु Kum. II. 13 they consider thee to be
Prakṛti, they know thee to be Purusha; कुमारं नेतारं कृता having
made Kumāra the leader (of the forces); नामु ततावुत्युजनामु अजं
चक्रार made his son Aja by name.

§ 799. † Intransitive roots govern the Accusative nouns
denoting space or country, or duration of time and distance;
कुच्युच्युच् युरुङु वसति युरुतुदं he stays in the country of the Kurus; तत्रु कति-
पुयुयुम् दिवुसं अवसत् there he dwelt for some days, गोदोहिकुयुयुल्युयु
he sits down till a cow is milked; कोशं प्रतिष्यु he walks for a
Koṣa, कोशो कुट्युटुटुला नदी the course of the river is winding for a
Koṣa; but sometimes factive studies whose length दीर्घुकोशं सत्युच्युयुङुयुदे नरुरु
the hill is situated at a part of a Koṣa.

§ 800 Roots having the sense of motion, real or metaphori-
cal govern the Accusative of the place to which it is directed.
ग्रामं गच्छुति goes to a village; अजवुयुचुरुयुङुयु विच्युचुत्युरु दाव्युदं with his
bow strung he roamed all over the forest; आनन्दुस्य परां काष्ठुमुष्टु-
गच्छुत they reached the highest point of joy; वनस्य कृष्णुयुङूतिं
goes to Kṛṣṇa (discus of him) mentally; अतः प्रभृति चिन्तुयुङोद्युच् स निद्रां
यथो while thus pondering he sank into sleep.

(a) When the motion is real, the Dative may also be used;
मातुलुय ग्रामं वा गच्छुति; but not of words denoting 'a road;'
