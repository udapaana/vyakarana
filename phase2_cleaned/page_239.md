(d) Two sets of personal terminations; the one called 'Parasmaipada' or the Verb Active:—as भवति 'he goes'; and the other 'Ātmanepada' or the verb Middle:—as वर्तते 'he is'. Some verbs take the former, others the latter, while others take either. Several roots again, though Parasmai, become Ātmanepada in some of the senses they take, or when certain prepositions are prefixed, or in particular tenses. These will be considered in treating of Ātmanepada and Parasmaipada.

(e) Derivative verbs are those formed from nouns and those primitive verbs which exist in the language, while primitive verbs are those which are derived from roots given in the Dhātupāṭhas or lexicon of roots.

(f) Primitive verbs are conjugated in the Active and Middle, and may be conjugated in the ten tenses and moods given above.

(g) Primitive verbs are conjugated in six different ways or conjugations:—

§ 379. (h) In each tense and mood there are three numbers, singular, dual and plural, with three persons in each.

§ 380. In § 378 (b) the first four are marked विकरण; the last six are called the Special Tenses (विकरणलकाराः) and the rest the Common Tenses or Lakāras (सकारान्तलकाराः). The special Tenses undergo peculiar modifications (विकरण) and these are therefore called Vikaraṇa-lakāras; the others Sakārānta-lakāras (सकारान्त).

§ 381.*

§ 382.* The first six lakāras are to be distinguished into two groups, the Special or Conjugational (विकरण) or general. In the former the characteristic marks and terminations of each are mostly added to a special base formed from the roots in various ways, while in the latter they are combined with the root itself.
