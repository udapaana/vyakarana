§ 790. (a) Sometimes an adjective does not agree with its
noun in gender &c. but is put in the neuter gender. This is an
idiom of the Sanskrit language: सर्वं देवदत्तः all Devadatta is,
स नाम महद् गुणौघोपेतः he was possessed of a great multitude of
virtues.

(b) When the adjective is put in the neuter gender it may
be used either in the singular or in the plural according to the
noun it qualifies सर्वाः स्त्रियः all the women, सर्वाणि सत्त्वानि sev-
eral living beings, &c.

§ 791. When an adjective is preceded by a word used in
the sense of 'a certain' or when it qualifies an adjective used
as a noun, it is treated as a substantive and generally put in the
neuter; किंचिद्रूपम् something beautiful, किंचिद् गम्भीरम् some-
thing deep, अल्पं गुरु something important, दृढतरम् something very
hard.

(a) But such expressions as सुरूपोऽयं, दृढरूपोऽयं are also
used.

§ 792. When an adjective qualifies a vocative noun, it
takes the case of the antecedent noun; or it agrees with its
noun in the vocative case. त्वयाऽस्य भोऽग्रज (त्वयो भोऽग्रज) O you
that are eldest brother, &c.

When it qualifies a noun used as a vocative but not in
the vocative case, the adjective is put in the vocative; हे
तृष्णिन् O thou greedy one, O thou thirsty one, हे कृष्ण O thou
black one, &c.

(a) The same applies to other kinds of addresses without
हे:—भो दृढिन् O you stubborn man, भो वत्स O my dear son.

(b) When adjectives are used adverbially with verbs, they
are put in the neuter gender in the accusative singular; शीघ्रं
तिष्ठति he stands swiftly, नीचं निपत्यते he falls down, दूरं गच्छति
he goes far. अस्य सर्वं समाप्तम् all this is finished, &c. So also
such expressions as क्षणं जीवति he lives for a moment, मुहुर्तं
तिष्ठति he stays for a moment, &c.
