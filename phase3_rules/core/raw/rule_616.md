§ 616. The Desiderative base is formed by reduplicating the
root or base according to the general rules of reduplication
given at § 444-448 and § 549 (c) (h), and by adding स् (which
may by changed to ष् according to the rules of Sandhi) to it.
The अ of the reduplicative syllable is changed to इ; e. g. चर्-
चचर्-चिचर्+स=चिचरिष् by the following rule.

N. B. The स् of a primitive root is not changed to ष् when
the characteristic स् is changed to ष्; सम्-सिस्मिष्, सिव्-सिस्विष्,
स्व-सुस्वः); स्ना-सिस्नासिष्; स्वप्-सुस्वप्सिष्; स्था-तिष्ठासा; स्थिव-तिष्ठीविष्,
स्फायि (सु); स्फारि; स्वप्, however, forms सुषुप्सिष्.

Note:—(1) A root takes a Desiderative form only when the
agent of the wish and of the action expressed by the root is the
same; so हिंस्यां पदन् इति इच्छति छः and not पिपदिषति; also the
sense of the root must be the object of the wish: गमनं इच्छति
cannot, therefore, be equal to जिगमिषति

Note:—(2) Though the Desiderative form of a root is not
to be met with often enough to assume a classical character as
a verb, yet nominal and participal derivatives from it are not
uncommonly to be met with.
