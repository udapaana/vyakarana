§ 808. † In the case of the causals of roots implying 'mo-
tion,' 'knowledge,' 'eating,' roots having some literary work for
their objects, intransitive roots and the roots हन् and जल्युट्, भूयु
with आङु; ह्युट् with हि, यङ्ट् and छु, their subject in the primitive
sense is put in the Accusative case.

शुचुद्युत्युगुयुयुयुर्त्युयुर्नों वैदुयुशं स्युवुक्तुनेदुयुद्युट् ।
आवासयुयुच्युयुवुं देवान्युच्युच्युदुच्युस्युयुयुदुहिचुचु ।
आयुयुयुयुत्युकुट्युङुङु पृथ्युश्यों यः ते स भोद्युहरेतोरुतुः ॥ Sid. Kan.

That venerable Hari is my refuge who despatched the enemies
(of gods) to the next world, explained the meaning of the
Vedas to his (followers), made the gods drink nectar, taught
Vedas to the Creator and seated the earth (made it rest)
on waters.

दैत्युयुनि हरिं मन्काद् he makes the devotees see Hari; जलुयुति
सुयुयुपुरे विद्युद्युयुयुदुत यत्युवुदुयुने यत्युङु मैत्युक्तुगुरुमुयोवुः (R. Kun. I. 82).
अध्युयुयुयुन्युरि यद्युयुन्कुयुष्युयुम्. But when the double causal is used the
subject ceases to be the subject of the root in the primitive
sense and therefore takes the Instrumental; रामुयुति देवदुत्तुः यद्युत्-
द्युत्तुं, गत्युयुति देवुदुत्तुनं यद्युद्युवुनं विष्णुयुदोदुवुतुः

(1) ‡ The same holds good in the case of the causal of अड् and
साहुङु; आदुयुति युवुदुवुनं ना अन्नं वुट्युनुं he makes the boy eat his food.

(2) § यट्, when it has not the sense of हिंसा 'pain or injury
to a sentient thing' does the same; भुक्तुयुयुच्युच्युन् वुट्युनुं; but भुक्तुयुति
वुछोरुवुदुदुरे सस्युयु (when the loss of corn causes pain to the mind).

(b) स्मृ and स्मार which denote a kind of knowledge are construed
with the Instrumental in the Atm. भोयुनं याद्युयुङोरुद्युवुचु when meaning
'to remember with pain' is sometimes found used with the
Accusative also; स्मरुयुति भीयुयुयुति वा देवुदुत्तुनं; अरिघु चैदुयुयुयुनोयु
अतिकुञ्युत्युयुषिश्युयुष्युयुकुनैरे स्मार्युयुन्ते प्रकुट्युतीः । Vide also Siṣ. VI. 66.

(c) § The causal of the denominative घुच्युयुरु also is construed
with the Instrumental; घुच्युयुवुयुति देवुदुत्तुनं he causes Devadatta
do make a sound.

Note:—¶ By intransitive roots are meant such roots as are
not capable of governing an object other than one denoting
space, time, &c. and not such as, though transitive, may be
used without their object being actually expressed; भारं आरुयुति
देवुदुत्तुं; but देवुदुत्तुं गच्युयुति and युदुत्युति though वुदुतुं and देवुदुत्तुं-
एवं is not actually expressed.
