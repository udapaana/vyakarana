§ 810 *When the causal forms are used in the passive, the
principal object (i. e. the primitive subject) is put in the
Nominative case; but in the case of roots implying 'knowledge
or eating' and roots having a literary work for their object,
the principal object is put in the Nominative case and the
secondary object in the Accusative or vice versa; देवुदुत्तुनं
कुरोति Devadatta prepares a mat—देवुदुत्तुं देवुदुत्तुनं वा क्तुटुं कारुयुति;
देवुदुत्तुः क्तुटुं कार्युते he makes Devadatta prepare a mat, देवुदुत्तुः ग्रामं
गच्युछुति Dev. goes to a village; देवुदुत्तुं ग्रामं गयुयुति causes Dev. to
दुयु युञु युत्युति मार्युयुवुकुः सूत्रुम् अध्युयुयुयुति Devadatta is made to
&c.; मागुयुवुकं यर्मं सोयुयुति he makes Māṇavaka know his duty;
योश्युयुते मागुयुकं यर्मः मापुयुकुयो यर्मं हेति वा Māṇavaka is made to
know, &c.; वुट्युयोदुनं भोजुयुते he makes the child eat food; वुट्यु-
रोदुनं भोज्युते or वुट्युयोदुनो भोज्युते the boy is made, &c.
