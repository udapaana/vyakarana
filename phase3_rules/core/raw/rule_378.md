§ 378. There are in Sanskrit—

(a) Two kinds of verbs, Primitive and Derivative.

(b) Six Tenses (कालः) and four Moods (अर्थः) which are as follow:—

TENSES   TECHNICAL NAME. MOODS TECHNICAL NAME.
वर्तमानः or Present        लट्     आज्ञा  or Imperative      लोट्
भूतः     or Aorist         लुङ्    विधि   or Potential विधिलिङ्
अनद्यतनभूतः or Imperfect   लङ्     आशीः or Benedictive आशीर्लिङ्
परोक्षभूतः or Perfect      लिट्    सत्ता  or Conditional लृङ्
अनद्यतन-}
भविष्यत्  } or 1 st Future लृट्
भविष्यत् or 2 nd Future   लुट्

लेट् or the Subjunctive is used only in the Veda and is therefore termed 'The Vedic Subjunctive.'

Note:—The ten tenses and moods are technically called the ten Lakaras in Sanskrit grammars.

(c) Three Voices (प्रयोगः), the Active Voice (कर्तरिप्रयोगः) as पचति, the Passive Voice (कर्मणिप्रयोगः), as पच्यते पक्ति यज्ञः, and the Impersonal Construction (भावेप्रयोगः) तेषां गम्यते.

(d) Two sets of personal terminations; the one called 'Parasmaipada' or the Verb Active:—as भवति 'he goes'; and the other 'Ātmanepada' or the verb Middle:—as वर्तते 'he is'. Some verbs take the former, others the latter, while others take either. Several roots again, though Parasmai, become Ātmanepada in some of the senses they take, or when certain prepositions are prefixed, or in particular tenses. These will be considered in treating of Ātmanepada and Parasmaipada.

(e) Derivative verbs are those formed from nouns and those primitive verbs which exist in the language, while primitive verbs are those which are derived from roots given in the Dhātupāṭhas or lexicon of roots.

(f) Primitive verbs are conjugated in the Active and Middle, and may be conjugated in the ten tenses and moods given above.
