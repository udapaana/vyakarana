§ 911. The infinitive in Sanskrit generally expresses the
purpose or that for which an action is done and thus corres-
ponds to the infinitive of purpose or gerund in English. The
infinitive in Sanskrit thus involves the sense of the Dative and
may, if desired, be replaced by the Dative of the verbal noun
derived from the root; यानीयु यात्युनै यदुद्युनुकुयुट् अवुतुरुयुरु descended
from the bank of Yamunā to drink water; here यात् may be re-
placed by यूनुयुयु (यूनोयुस्युयु यूनुयु); शुच्छुयूरोविनुस्युयुयुयुयुकुनै (Rag.
X. 25.) where गोकुच् =गोगुयुयु.

"The infinitive (formed with तुयु tum) in Sanskrit?" remarks
Prof. Monier Williams, "cannot be employed with the same
latitude as in other languages. Its use is very limited cor-
responding to that of the Latin supine, as its termination tum
indicates."

(a) "Let the student, therefore, distinguish between the in-
finitive of Sanskrit and that of Latin and Greek. In these latter
languages we have the infinitive made the subject of a proposi-
tion, or, in other words, standing in the place of a Nominative
and an Accusative case often admissible before it. We have it
also assuming different forms, to express present, past, or future



time, and completeness or incompleteness in the progress of
the action. The Sanskrit infinitive, on the other hand, can
never be made the subject of a verb, admits of no Accusative
before it, and can only express indefiniteness time and incom-
plete action. Wherever it occurs, it must be considered as the
object, and never the subject of some verb expressed or under-
stood. As the object of the verb, it may be regarded as
equivalent to a verbal substantive, in which the force of two
cases, an Accusative and Dative, is inherent and which
differs from other substantives in its power of governing a case.
Its use as a substantive, with the force of the Accusative case,
corresponds to our use of the Latin infinitive; thus तवु युयु आदुङु
युयुयुयुयु is I desire to hear thou that, तत्तुयु यात्युयु; where युस्युयु युयु आदुट्
and audive are both equivalent to Accusative. Similarly, रोदितुयूदु
युदुरुयुयु 'she began to weep,' and यहुयो जोतुदुयु आयुयुनै, he began to conquer
the earth, where मुहोजुयुयुनै भुयूयुनै, he began the conquest of the earth,
would be equally correct.''

(b) "Bopp considers the termination of the infinitive to be
the Accusative of the suffix tu (§ 453, Obs.), and it is certain
that in the veda other cases of nouns formed with this suffix in
the sense of the infinitive occur; e.g. a Dative in tave, havai, se
from han comes hantave 'to kill;' fr, anu-i entease, 'to follow;'
fr, man, mantavai, 'to think;' there is also a form in tos generally
in the sense of an Ablative; e.g. fr. i, comes stos 'from going;'
ir, han, hantos, 'from killing,' 'before killing;' and a form in
tsi corresponding to the indeclinable participle in tvā of the
classical languages; e.g. fr. han, hatsi 'killing,' fr. bhā bhātsi
'being,' &c &c," Sanskrit Grammar.
