§ 3. The nine primary vowels consist of five simple vowels
viz. अ, इ, उ, ऋ and ऌ, and four diphthongs viz. ए, ऐ,
ओ and औ.

(a) A vowel may be ह्रस्व or short, दीर्घ or long, [?] द्रुत or
protracted (also called prolated by some) according to the
time required to pronounce it. * The vowels are thus divided

(1) Short vowels—अ a, इ i, उ u, ऋ ṛ, ऌ ḷ;
(2) Long vowels—आ ā, ई ī, ऊ ū, ऋ e, ऐ ai, ओ o, औ
au; and.

(3) Protracted vowels आ ३ ā 3, ई ३ ī 3, ऊ ३, ऊ ३ ṛ 3
ऌ ३ ḷ 3, ए ३ e 3, ऐ ३ ai 3, ओ ३ o 3, and औ ३ au 3.

N. B.—As the Pluta or protracted vowels are not commonly
to be met with in classical Sanskrit, the vowels are usually
given as thirteen, represented by the thirteen signs given above
under (1) and (2).

(b) Each of these vowels may be again of two kinds, अनु-
नासिक or nasalised, and अननुनासिक or without a nasal sound.†

(c) Vowels are also further discriminated into उदात्त or
acute, अनुदात्त or grave, and स्वरित or circumflex. उदात्त is

articular vowel: e. g. अ means अ, आ and आ ३, but अनु means
अ (of six kinds) only; so कृत means कृ and nothing else.
