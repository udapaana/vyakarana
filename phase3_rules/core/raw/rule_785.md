§ 785. The predicate may not always be a finite verb; but
a participle, or an adjective or a noun may take its place.

(a) When a participle is used as the predicate it must
agree with the subject in number and gender; स वदन्तमानं
said that, स वकुक्तवती she said that, तेषां कृतानानि बन्धानि their
bonds were cut off, कृतं छत्त the work is done, छिन्ना लता the
creeper is cut, &c.

(b) When an adjective or a noun is used as the predicate,
a form of the roots अस् or भू may be used with it or may be
omitted; the adjective used predicatively agrees with the
subject in number and gender; words like आत्मन्, पत्त्र, भावना,
स्थान, प्रा &c. retain their gender and number; सुशयोः दुर्लभः a
good servant is difficult to be obtained, सुप्रयः पित्राः गैर्व्यवानामुव च
good sons are the object of his father's pride, समदः पदं विपस्तो
riches are the abode of miseries, स तु तेषां अभिमानवर्धनो भवति
in these cases the verb agrees with the subject in number and
not with the noun used predicatively; सत्पुरुष आधारवर्मो पदं सन्ति
and not अस्ति, &c.
