#### § 26. No @[sandhi] is possible in the case of—

1 What are called the @[pragṛhya] exceptions; viz.—

(a) When a dual form (whether of a noun, pronoun or verb) ends in @[ī], @[ū], or @[e]; e. g. @[harī etau]; @[viṣṇū imau]; @[te ete]; @[te imau].

- @[go strī yutir dvayoḥ upasaṃkhyānam] | @[adhvapramāṇe ca] | Vārttikas.

† @[cayya jayyau śakyārthe] | Pan. VII. 1. 81.

‡ @[eto'ṃ] Pan. VI. 1. 109. § @[sarvatra vibhāṣā goḥ] | @[akṣe sphoṭāyanasya] | @[indre ca] | Pan. VI. 1. 122-24.

¶ @[plutapragṛhyā aci nityam] &c. VI. 1. 125.

|| @[pragṛhyasaṃjñā] Pan. I. 1. 11.

(e) When ई or ऊ follows the अ of the pronoun अदस् (§ 55); e. g. अमी ईशाः these lords; अमू आसाते the two sit down.

Obs. The ए of the Vedic forms अस्मे and युष्मे also does not blend with a following vowel (अचि र ९. @[Pāṇ]. I. 1. 11); e. g. अस्मे इन्द्राबृहस्पती &c. @[Ṛg]. I. 49. 4. Similarly a Vedic word having the sense of the loc. (but not ending in the termination of the loc.) remains unaffected by a following vowel; सोमो गौरी अधि श्रितः &c. Ṛg. I. 12. 3. where गौरी stands for गौर्याम् the loc. term. being dropped by षट्सुं सुलुक् Pāṇ. VII. 1. 39.

(f) Particles consisting of a single vowel, except आ meaning 'a little, or a limit' exclusive or inclusive or used as a preposition, when followed by a vowel, e. g. ई इन्द्र Oh Indra. ऊ गव, आ एवं मन्यसे ah, you think so; but आ + उष्णम् = आऽऽउष्णम् (a little warm) &c.

(g) When an indeclinable ends in ओ; e. g. अहो ईशाः. The final ओ of a noun in the vocative case optionally combines when followed by इति; e. g. विष्णो इति = विष्णविति, and also विष्णो इति (see § 24 a.).

N. B. All these vowels which do not blend with others, and sometimes the words in which they occur are technically called @[pragrihya].
