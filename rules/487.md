**(4) THE PERFECT (@[liṭ])**

**§ 488.** There are two kinds of perfect, Reduplicative and Periphrastic.

**§ 489.** The Reduplicative perfect is formed of all monosyllabic roots beginning with consonants, as well as of those beginning with the vowels @[a] or @[ā], and @[i], @[u], and @[ṛ] short.

Exceptions—@[aj], @[ay], @[kās], @[kram]. These take the Periphrastic perfect necessarily.

**§ 490.** The Periphrastic perfect is formed of all roots beginning with any vowel except @[a] or @[ā] which is naturally or prosodially long, and of roots of more than one syllable (roots of the 10th class and other derivative roots included). Exceptions:—@[īkṣ] and @[īrch] which take the Reduplicative Perfects.

**§ 491.** The roots @[uṣ], @[vad], @[vaś], @[mī], @[vī], @[vṛ], @[hṛ], and @[daridr] take both the perfects.
