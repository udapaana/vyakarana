Atm. उत्पते or वितपते इत्यं:; when used transitively it is Atm. if it has a limb of the body of the agent for its object; उत्पते or वितपते पाणिं 'he warms his hand, but उत्पति सुवर्णं सुवर्णकार: 'a goldsmith heats gold', चैत्रो मैत्रस्य पाणिमुतपति; when it means 'to practise penance' it is Atm. and is conjugated like a root of the fourth class.

According to some तप् with अनु is Atm; अनुतपते 'repents.'

दा—*'to give' (3 cl.) by itself takes either pada; but when preceded by आ it is Atm. in any other sense than opening (the mouth, &c.); धनं आदत्ते 'accepts money,' विद्यां आदत्ते 'acquires knowledge,' नादत्ते भवतां स्नेहेन या पहुवं (Śāk.) 'does not pluck your foliage through affection,' but मुखं व्यादद्दाति 'opens his mouth,' विपादिकां व्यादद्दाति वैद्य: 'the doctor opens the tumour on the root,' नदीकूलं व्यादद्दाति 'breaks open the bank of a river,' but if the mouth belongs to another the exception is removed; व्यादद्दते पतङ्गस्य मुखं पिपीलिका: 'ants break open the mouth of a moth' (Māh. Bhār.).

दा—'to give' (1. cl.) when preceded by the preposition सम् singly or coupled with any other preposition takes the Atm. provided it is used with the Instrumental in the sense of the Dative; दास्या संप्रच्छते or संप्रयच्छते 'gives (something) to the maid-servant,' but दास्या धनं संप्रयच्छति विप्राय 'gives wealth to a Brāhmaṇa through his maid-servant.'

दृश्—'to see preceded by उप and used intransitively takes the Atm.; उपप्श्यते 'sees (thinks) well;' this root takes the Atm. in the Desiderative: दिदृक्षते 'wishes to see.'

धु—'to run' is Par. in the Cau.

नह्—with सम् is Atm. in the sense of 'preparing, being ready
