§ 773. *A root may be used in its gerundive form in अय् 
with its object prefixed to it if that object be a part of the body
which can be severed without fatal effects; भ्रुविक्षेपं कथयति 'narrates contracting the eyebrows; but शिर उद्धिक्षेप् (not शिरउद्धिक्षेपय्)
as the severance of the head would cause death

(a) Similarly when a part of the body is completely hurt
or pressed in the action, the gerund in अय् is used with that
part; उर:प्रतिपेषं कुर्वन्ते i.e. कृत्स्नउर: पीडयन्त: 'afflicting the
whole bosom;' उरोविहारं प्रतिवन्ध्य नक्षे ।

§ 774. †The roots विश्, पत्, पद्, and स्कन्द् are used in their
gerundive forms in अय् in the sense of 'complete occupation or
pervasion or repetition' with nouns which if not compounded
would have stood in the Acc.; गेहानुप्रवेशमास्ते गेहं गेहमनुप्रविश्य ।
गेहमनुप्रवेशमनुप्रवेशम् । so गेहानुप्पातम्, गेहानुप्पादम्, गेहानु-
स्कन्दम्, &c.

§ 775. (a)‡ The gerunds of अस् and वृत् are used with nouns
denoting a period of time intervening between the repetition or
performance of the action; द्व्यहास्यात् or द्व्यहवृत्यात् गा: पाययति 'he
makes the cows drink water allowing two days to intervene,
i.e. every third day' अय पाययित्वा द्व्यमहतिक्रम्य पुन: पाययतीत्यर्थ: ।
(Sid. Kau.); similarly द्राहास्येष or द्राहवृत्येष.
