## § 616. Formation of the Desiderative Base

The Desiderative base is formed by reduplicating the root or base according to the general rules of reduplication given at @ref[§ 444-449] and @ref[§ 549 (a) (b)], and by adding @deva[स] (which may be changed to @deva[ष] according to the rules of Sandhi) to it. The @deva[अ] of the reduplicative syllable is changed to @deva[इ]; e.g. @deva[पठ्-पठ्+स्=पिपठिष्] by the following rule.

**N. B.** The @deva[स] of a primitive root is not changed to @deva[ष] when the characteristic @deva[स] is changed to @deva[ष]; @deva[सि-सिसीष्], @deva[सिच्-सिसिक्ष्] (@deva[क+स]); @deva[सि-सिस्वपिष्]; @deva[स्व-सुष्वप्]; but @deva[स्था-तिष्ठास्]; @deva[सा-सिसाष्] can. of @deva[स्-सुषावपिष्]; @deva[स्वद्], however, forms @deva[सुसूषति].

**Note:**—(1) A root takes a Desiderative form only when the agent of the wish and of the action expressed by the root is the same; so @deva[शिष्यः पठन्तु इति इच्छति गुरुः] and not @deva[पिपठिषति]; also the sense of the root must be the object of the wish: @deva[भमनेन इच्छति] cannot, therefore, be equal to @deva[निनमिषति].

**Note:**—(2) Though the Desiderative form of a root is not to be found often used in classical language in its character of a verb, yet nominal and participial derivatives from it are not uncommonly to be met with.
