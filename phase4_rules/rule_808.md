## § 808. Causals of certain verbs govern Accusative of primitive subject

In the case of[^1] the causals of roots implying 'motion,' 'knowledge,' 'eating,' roots having some literary work for their objects, intransitive roots and the roots @deva[हन्] and @deva[जल्प], @deva[भूय] with @deva[आङ्]; @deva[ह्वृ] with @deva[हि], @deva[यङ्] and @deva[ल], their subject in the primitive sense is put in the Accusative case.

@deva[शुचित्युगुयूर्युनस् वैदुशम् स्वुक्तुनेदुयत् ।
आवासयुच्युवुम् देवान्युच्च्युदुच्युस्युयुदुहिचुचु ।
आयुयुयुत्युकुयुङुम् पृथ्युश्यस् यः ते स भोद्युहरेतोरुतुः ॥] Sid. Kau.

That venerable Hari is my refuge who despatched the enemies (of gods) to the next world, explained the meaning of the Vedas to his (followers), made the gods drink nectar, taught Vedas to the Creator and seated the earth (made it rest) on waters.

e.g. @deva[दैत्युनि हरिम् मन्कात्]: he makes the devotees see Hari.

@deva[जलयति सुय्युपरे विद्युद्युयुदुत यत्युवुदुयुने यत्युङु मैत्युकुगुरुमुयोवुः] (R. Kum. I. 82).

@deva[अध्युयुयुन्युरि यत्युयुन्कुयुष्युयुम्]. But when the double causal is used the subject ceases to be the subject of the root in the primitive sense and therefore takes the Instrumental; @deva[रामयति देवदत्तः यत्युत्द्युत्तुम्], @deva[गत्युयुति देवुदुत्तुनम् यत्युद्युवुनम् विष्णुयुदोदुवुतुः].

**Obs.**—@deva[याच्] is sometimes found used with the Dative; @deva[यत्युयुविम्ङुयोगुरुलम् स द्युङुकुङुतिर्युयु] Ragh. XII. 54.

(a) The causals of @deva[नी], and @deva[वह्] when it has for its subject a noun other than one denoting a driver, govern the Instrumental of the primitive subject;[^2] @deva[नाययति वाहयति वा भारम् यरथेनम्]: he makes his servant carry a load; @deva[युरञ्चयति रथम् वाहानम् यत्तुः]: the charioteer makes the horses draw the chariot.

(1) The same holds good in the case of the causal of @deva[अट्] and @deva[साहुङ्];[^3] @deva[आदयति युवुदुवुनम् ना अन्नम् वुयुनुम्]: he makes the boy eat his food.

(2) @deva[यट्], when it has not the sense of @deva[हिंसा] 'pain or injury to a sentient thing' does the same;[^4] @deva[भुक्तुयुच्युच्युन् वुयुनुम्]; but @deva[भुक्तुयुति वुलोरुवुदुदुरे सस्युयु] (when the loss of corn causes pain to the mind).

(b) @deva[स्तु] and @deva[ब्रू] which denote a kind of knowledge are construed with the Instrumental in the Atm. @deva[भोयुनम् याद्युयुङोरुद्युवुचु] when meaning 'to remember with pain' is sometimes found used with the Accusative also; @deva[स्मरयति भीयुयुयुति वा देवुदुत्तुनम्]; @deva[अरिघु चैदुयुयुयुनोयु अतिकुञ्युत्युयुषिश्युयुष्युयुकुनैरे स्मार्युयुन्ते प्रकुयुतीः]. Vide also Siṣ. VI. 66.

(c) The causal of the denominative @deva[घोष्यु] also is construed with the Instrumental;[^5] @deva[घोष्यवुयुति देवुदुत्तुनम्]: he causes Devadatta do make a sound.

**Note:**—By intransitive roots are meant[^6] such roots as are not capable of governing an object other than one denoting space, time, &c. and not such as, though transitive, may be used without their object being actually expressed; @deva[भारम् आरुयुति देवुदुत्तुम्]; but @deva[देवुदुत्तुम् गच्युयुति] and @deva[युदुत्युति] though @deva[वुदुतुम्] and @deva[देवुदुत्तुम्-एवम्] is not actually expressed.
