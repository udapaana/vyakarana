## § 221. Upamāna Karmadhāraya Compounds

(a) A word expressive of the standard of comparison (@deva[उपमान]) may be compounded in a Karmadhāraya with another denoting the common quality or ground of comparison;[^1] as @deva[घन इव श्यामः] @deva[घनश्यामः] dark like a cloud. Such compounds are called @deva[उपमानपूर्वपदकर्मधारयम्].

(b) Similarly a noun denoting the person or thing compared (@deva[उपमेय]) may be compounded with words indicative of excellence, such, as @deva[व्याघ्र], @deva[सिंह], @deva[चन्द्र], @deva[कमल], etc.[^2] no mention of the common attribute (of similarity) being made;[^3] as @deva[पुरुषौ व्याघ्रः इव] @deva[पुरुषव्याघ्रः] a man like a tiger (i.e. as bold as a tiger); @deva[मुखं चन्द्रः इव] @deva[मुखचन्द्रः] a moon-like face i.e. as delightful as the moon; @deva[मुखं कमलम् इव] @deva[मुखकमलम्] etc.; these are called @deva[उपमानोत्तरपदकर्मधारय].

**Note 1.**—The difference between the two is this:—In the former the common quality of comparison is actually expressed; while in the latter it is left to be understood; in fact if it be expressed there can be no compound; as @deva[पुरुषः व्याघ्रः इव शूरः].

**Note 2.**—The above Karmadhārayas may be dissolved as @deva[मुखम् एव चन्द्रः] @deva[मुखचन्द्रः], @deva[मुखम् एव कमलम्] @deva[मुखकमलम्], etc. Dissolved in either way there will be no difference in the formation of the compounds; but there will be difference both in the meaning and in the metaphor. In one case prominence will be given to @deva[चन्द्र] or the moon and the figure will be 'upamā (a simile),' while in the other to @deva[मुख] or the face in which case the figure will be 'Rūpaka.' Compounds like @deva[पादः एव पद्मम्] @deva[पादपद्म], @deva[विद्या एव धनम्] @deva[विद्याधनम्], etc. are termed @deva[अव्ययीभावपूर्वपदकर्मधारयम्].
