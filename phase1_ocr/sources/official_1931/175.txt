(c)* दन्त necessarily when it is preceded by a numeral or सु indicating age, or when the whole compound forms a feminine name; and optionally when it is preceded by स्फुाद्, अर्घ, a word ending in अम, शुद्ध, शुघ, टष, or वराह; द्वौ दन्तौ यस्य स द्विदन्त 'a child so small as to have two teeth only,' पड़ दन्ता अस्य । षोदन्त; शोभना दन्ता अस्य सुदन्त, सुदन्ती (i. e. youthful) fem.; but द्विदन्तः कषी, सुदन्तः (having beautiful teeth) पुरुष, अयोदन्ती, फलदन्ती (both proper names, ) &c. but समदन्ती having even rows of teeth, श्यावा (black) दन्ता यस्य स श्यावदन्त-न्तः; अरोष्टदन्त-दन्तः having black or thick-set teeth; कुम्भडुलाश्वदन्त-न्तः, having teeth like the points of buds, शुक्रवदन्त-न्तः &c.

(d) ‡ ककुद् 'when the compound denotes a particular stage of growth;' अजातं ककुदं यस्य सः अजातककुद् 'a bull that has not got his hump as yet' i. e. 'who is very young'; also when it is preceded by त्रि and the compound designates the mountain of that name; as त्रिककुद् 'name of a mountain with three peaks'; but त्रिककुदः. 'having three humps'.

(e) § कालुद् 'the palate' (कालुदं ताल् Kās.) necessarily when preceded by उद् and त्रि and optionally when preceded by पूर्ण; as उत्कालुद्, त्रिकालुद्; पुणेंकालुदू-लुदः.

§ 258. || हृदय is changed to हृद् when preceded by सु or दुश् meaning 'a friend', or an 'enemy' respectively; सुर्भं हृदयं यस्य स सुहृद् 'a friend', दुष्टं हृ दुर्हृद् 'an enemy'; but सुहृदयः 'one who has got a good heart' i. e. 'one who is able to appreciate merits', &c; दुर्हृदयः 'a man of wicked intentions.'
