Atm. उत्तपते or विततपे सूर्य: when used transitively it is Atm.
If it has a limb of the body of the agent for its object; इत्तपते
or विततपे पान्नि ' he warms his hands. but सूर्य: सुवर्णासारु
or सुवर्णङ् सूर्यं  he warms his hand,  but स्वर्य: सुवर्णाङ्गतपति: when  it means
'to practise penance' it is Atm. and  is conjugated like a root
of the fourth class.

According to some दृप् with अनु is Atm; अनुतपते 'repents,'

दा—*'to give ' ( 3 cl. ) by itself takes either pada;  but when-
preceded by आ it is Atm. in any other sense than opening ( the
mouth, &c. ); पदं आदत्ते  'accepts money;'  विद्या आदत्ते  'acquires
knowledge;' नादते भवता रुद्वेऽ या पहुट्व  ( Sāk. ) ' does not pluck
your foliage through devotion;' but गात्रं आदान: स्यायाति  'opens
mouth ; ' द्वीत्वोकं सातेति शुद : ' the doctor opens the tumour on
the root;' नदीद्वुरं स्यादद्वाति  ' breaks open the bank of a river;'
but if the mouth belongs to another the exception is removed;
स्यादद्वते पत्रकुस्य मुखं पिपीलिका: 'ants break open the mouth of a
moth' ( Mah. Bha. ).

दा†—'to give' ( I cl. ) when preceded by  the preposition  सत्य
simply or coupled with any other  preposition  takes the Atm.
provided it is used with the Instrumental in the sense of the
Dative; दासरा सुनुस्यत्ते or संपदस्यत्ते ' gives ( something ) to the
maid-servant,'  but दासरा धन संपदस्यदति विद्यां ' gives  wealth to a.
Brāhman through the maid-servant '

दृश्‡—'to see preceded by उप and used intransitively takes the
Atm.; स्वर्गं ङेति इति-क वेद्य ; ' this root takes the Atm. in
the Desiderative; दिदृक्षते 'wishes to see.'
