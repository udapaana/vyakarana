§ 785.  The predicate may not always  be a  finite  verb;  but
a participle, or an adjective or a noun may take its place.

(a)  When a participle  is  used  as  the  predicate  it  must
agree with the subject in  number  and  gender;  स  वदन्कुदानं
said that, स वदुक्तवती she said that, तेषां कत्यमानि छिद्राति their
bonds were cut  off,  कृतं  छत्तं  the  work  is  done, छुता छिन्ना the
creeper is cut, &c.

(b)  When an adjective or a noun is used  as  the  predicate,
a form of the roots अस् or भू may  be  used  with it  or  may be
omitted; the  adjective  used  predicatively  agrees  with  the
subject in number and gender;  words  like  आत्मनु,  पत्रं,  भावना,
स्थानु, प्रा &c. retain  their  gender  and  number;  सुशुयुयुः  हुटुभुः a
good servant is difficult to be  obtained,  सुप्रयुः  पित्युः  गवाल्ब्युयुव युच
good sons are the object  of  his  father's  pride,  समदुः  पदुः  पुस्तो
riches are the abode  of  miseries,  स  तु  तेषां  अभिमानवर्धनो भवति
in these cases the verb  agrees  with  the  subject  in  number and
not with the noun  used  predicatively;  सत्पुरुदु  आयुयुर्वो  पदं  सन्ति
and not अस्ति, &c.

§ 786.  When a substantive or an adjective is used  predica-
tively with verbs of incomplete predication  such as  युद्  to grow,
to seem, to be, to appear,' &c.  the  substantive  or  adjective
used must agree with the subject in case;  एषं मे  विश्युयुयुरुः  this is
my  resolve;  सः  श्युयुतोरुः  प्रजानरुह्नोः छुच्छुयुर्  that  king  seems
wakened  through  wakefulness; नयुत्युड्युप्रुट्युड्युयुनयुरुयुयु  desirous of
taking hold of the three worlds.

(a)  The  same  applies  to  transitive  roots  of  incomplete
predication used  passively; स्वं च मुनिर्  वैगारिकः  कुटुतोः  कुतु.  by
that sage the mouse was made ( transformed  into )  a cat,  &c.;
इयुगो हि  विष्णुयुःु सत्युगरे  a  king  is thought to be  Viṣṇu.
