                        I. PREPOSITIONS.

§ 365. A preposition, styled 'Upasarga or Gāti,' in Sanskrit is
an indeclinable word, having an independent meaning and prefixed
to verbs and also to their derivatives. These prepositions modify,
intensify and sometimes totally alter the senses of roots†;

* सदृशं त्रिषु लिङ्गेषु सर्वासु च विभक्तिषु ।
वचनेषु च सर्वेषु यन् न व्येति तदव्ययम् ॥
स्वराडिनिपतमव्ययम् । Pān. I, 1. 37.
† धास्थये वाधते कञ्चित्कञ्चिन्नमनुवर्तते ।
तत्रैव विधीनष्यन्य उपसर्गेगातिविक्रिया ॥
Cf. also Sid. Kau.    उपसर्गेण धात्वर्थो बलाद्‌ऽन्यन्न्त्र नीयते ।
                    प्रहाराहारसंहारविहारपरिहारवत् ॥
Some think that prepositions have no meaning of their own,
but they simply bring to light when prefixed to roots their
hidden senses. ( cf. Sis. X, 15 ).
