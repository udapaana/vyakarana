The Prātipadika, when it is inflected, is called a Pada or a word. A noun admits of inflection in seven cases, in the three num-bers Singular ( वचन or एकवचन ) Dual ( द्विवचन ) and Plural ( बहुवचन ) The nominative case is called प्रथमा the second द्वितीया, the third तृतीया, the fourth चतुर्थी, the fifth पञ्चमी, the sixth षष्ठी, and the seventh सप्तमी.

§ 53. The indeclinables, i. e. words that are incapable of case-affixes, are participles ( अव्यय ), such as च and, यदि if, यदा when, किं why. These, as distinguished from ordinary nouns, are therefore technically called Nip&tas or Avyayas. The word Pada is applied to such words as well.

§ 54. Substantives (संज्ञा). masculine (पुंलिङ्ग), femi-nine (स्त्रीलिङ्ग), and neuter (नपुंसकलिङ्ग)* and adjectives (विशेषण) may be classified as follows:—

1. Words ending in short अ. ( the short  अ being changed to a long one in the Nominative and vocative singular ).

(a) Masculine and neuter, e.g. (m) नरः (voc. नर ) a man; (n) कुसुमम् a flower.

(b) Feminine, e.g. कन्या a girl, बाला a girl; in this class adjectives also end in अ and not in आ; e.g. विद्वद्बाला a learned girl.

II. Words ending in short इ:— (a) M. f and n.; कवि m. a poet, मति f. intellect, वारि n. water.

(b) Feminine words ending in ई do not differ in inflection from those ending in इ except that they have ई in the Nom. Acc.

* Noun ending in अ are masculine, feminine and neuter; those ending in आ are feminine; those ending in इ are generally masculine and those ending in ई generally feminine. Feminine words ending in इ change their इ into ई in forming the Nominative of the Dual. Short इ or उ at the end of a noun is made long in the Vocative Singular. Adjectives compounded with the words, उच्च tall, नीच low and प्रिय fond of are neuter when the meaning is not living. But compounds of प्रिय are masculine or feminine when the sense is living &c. e.g. देवप्रियम् dear to a god, (a flower, neuter), देवप्रियः dear to a god (a person, mas.).
