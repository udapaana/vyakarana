470                  SANSKRIT GRAMMAR                   [ § 780

(a)  The singular may be used to denote a  class;  सिंहः
भावदरान: the lion is the king of beasts; मुक्तिकमुग्र नरः श्रेष्ठः &c.

(b) The word समान sometimes denotes the whole of the
same class; पितरो parents; चाटुको a male and a female of sparrow.

(1)  Note:—Words like द्वं, द्वितीयं, द्वा, द्वं, &c.  meaning
'a pair,' &c  which are dual in sense but singular  in  form ought
to be always used in the  singular,  except  when  several  pairs
are meant.

(2)  Note:—Words like हस्तो, नेत्रे, पादो, &c. should always
be used in the dual in Sanskrit.

(e)  The plural, like  the  singular,  may  represent  a  class,
ब्राह्मणाः पूज्याः ('or ब्राह्मणः पूज्यः') Brāhmaṇas (i. e. the Brāhmaṇa
class) are adorable.

(1) The plural is not  unfrequently  used  as  a  mark of res-
pect or reverence.  भवते श्रीशङ्कराचार्याः so ( says )  the venerable
Śankarāchārya; भूति  आचार्यगुणाः  this  is  the  opinion  of  the
reverend preceptor, &c.

(2)  In the first person,  great personages and writers  some-
times  use  the  plural  instead  of  the  singular;  वयम्पि  भवन्तो
किमपि पुच्छामः we ( i. e. I, ) too ask  you  something;  भूतं  तु  वयं
but we (i. e. I, the writer )  hold this opinion;  वयम्पि  च  निरा-
क्षिमेः we rule over speech ( i. e. language ).

(3)  Words like द्युतः, यद्वाः, अयंदाः, सिक्काः, आयुः, गुणाः, द्वाजाः,
&c. are always used in the plural,  though  some  of  them  are
singular in sense.

(4)   Names of countries which are  really the names  of  the
people inhabiting them, must be used in the plural, स  वविदेहान्
उपपत्येर he went to Videha, &c.

But in the  case  of  compounds  ending  in  words  denoting a
country, such as देश, विषय, &c.  the  sing  must  be  used;  आसीत्
मगधेषो पाटलिपुत्रं नाम नगरं there  is  a  town called  Pātalipụtra
in the country of Magadha.

(5)  The plural of proper nouns denotes  a  family  or  race
as in English; जनकानां रघूणां च वंकस्तं गोत्रमङ्कुतः Utta.
