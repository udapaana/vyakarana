§ 614  The Desiderative expresses the notion that a person
or thing wishes ( or is about ) to perform the action, or to be
in the condition denoted by the root or the desiderative base;
विपठिषति ' he wishes to study ' from पठ् ; मुमूर्षति ' he is about
to die ' fr. मृ ; &c.

615.  There are a few primitive roots, which, though they
take a Desiderative form, do not convey a desiderative sense
( See § 39. b ). As has been looked upon as primitive roots,
the Desiderative bases may be derived from them according
to the rules given below; as जुगुप्सिषत ' he wishes to censure '
from जुगुप्स् &c.

§ 616.  The Desiderative base is formed by reduplicating the
root or base according to the general rules of reduplication
given at § 444-448 and § 549 (c) (h), and by adding स् ( which
may by changed to य् according to the rules of Sandhi ) to it.
The अ of the reduplicative syllable is changed to इ; e  g.  चर्-
चचर्-विचर्+स=चिचरिष् by the following rule.

N. B.  The स् of a primitive root is not changed to य् when
the characteristic स् is changed to स् ; स्म-सिस्मिष्, सिस्व-सिस्विष्,
स्व-सुस्वः ); स्ना-सिस्नासिष् ; स्वप्-सुस्वप्सिष् ; स्था-तिष्ठासा; स्थिव-तिष्ठीविष्,
स्फायि ( सु ); स्फारि; स्वप्, however, forms सुषुप्तिष्.

Note:—(1) A root takes a Desiderative form only when the
agent of the wish and of the action expressed by the root is the
same; so हिंस्या: पदन् इति इच्छति छः and not पिपदिषति; also the
sense of the root must be the object of the wish: गमनं इच्छति
cannot, therefore, be equal to जिगमिषति

Note:—(2)  Though the Desiderative form of a root is not
to be met with often enough to assume a classical character as
a verb, yet nominal and participal derivatives from it are not
uncommonly to be met with.
