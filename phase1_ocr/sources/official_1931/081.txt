§ 107. The neuter forms are प्राक् in nom., voc., and acc.
sing., like स्वदृष्ट and प्राञ्ची in nom., voc., and acc. pl. like
वारि ( वा० II. 4. 4 ; Pan. VI. 1. 96, VII. 1. 70 ). Other forms
are similar to those of the masculine. The forms of fem. and
neuter of सम्यञ्च् &c. can be formed by the student himself.

                Bases ending in त् or द् ॥

§ 108. ( 1 ) Consonantal bases ending in त् or द् drop
this final letter in the nom. sing, either as being followed by
the special स् which is dropped, but produces the dropping of
त् or द् by the rule नाम्यपरे ( Pan. VIII. 2. 7 ), or, द् or त् is
dropped by the rule हलन्त्यम् ( Pan. VIII. 2. 23 ), their place being
supplied ( optionally when initial, and necessarily when non-
initial ) by the सवर्ण of the preceding vowel ( Pan. VI. 1. 68 )
if it be short ( ह्रस्व ); thus भगवत् ( happy or adorable ) takes the
nom. ending स्; द् becomes त् before स् by virtue of the rule
झलां जशोऽन्ते ( Pan. VIII. 2. 39 ); this त् and स् are
optionally dropped if the vowel before be long, and necessar-
ily dropped if it be short, by हलन्त्यम् ( VIII. 2. 23 ); and thus
from भगवत् + स् we have भगवान् in which अ + अ become
आ ; but from जगत् we get only जगत् in the nom. sing., the final
consonant not being dropped. It will be seen that when त्
or द् is final, i. e., when there is no ending, it is retained in
its place being not subject to the dropping rule. So we
have विद्वत् ( learned ) and सत् ( existent, real, good ) in nom.
