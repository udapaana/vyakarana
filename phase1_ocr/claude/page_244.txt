Chapter XII.

                       CONJUGATION OF VERBS.
§ 378. There are in Sanskrit—
(a) Two kinds of verbs, Primitive and Derivative.
(b) Six Tenses (कालाः) and four Moods (अथाः) which are
as follows:—

TENSES    TECHNICAL NAME   MOODS   TECHNICAL NAME.
वर्तमानः or Present        लट्    आज्ञा  or Imperative   लोट्
लुङ्     or Aorist         लुङ्   विधि  or Potential   विधिलिङ्
अनद्यतनभूतः or Imperfect   लङ्    आशीः or Benedictive आशीर्लिङ्
परोक्षभूतः or Perfect      लिट्   सङ्केत or Conditional लृङ्
अनद्यतन-  }
भविष्यत्   } or 1 st Future लुट्
भविष्यत् or 2 nd Future    लृट्:

लेट् or the Subjunctive is used only in the Veda and is
therefore termed 'The Vedic Subjunctive.'

Note:—The ten tenses and moods are technically called the
ten Lakaras in Sanskrit grammars.

(c) Three Voices (प्रयोगाः), the Active Voice (कर्तरिप्र०) as
रामः सत्यं भाषते, the Passive Voice (कर्मणिप्र०), as हरिणा फलं भ-
क्षयते, and the Impersonal Construction (भावेप्र०) रामेण गम्यते.

* These technical names are given in the following Kārikā.

लट् वर्तमाने लेट् वेदे सेटे लुट्लृट्लिटस्तथा।
विध्याशिषोश्च लिङ्लोटौ लृङ् लुङ् लुङ् च भविष्यति॥

This terminology of Pāṇini, it will be seen, is artificial.
Other grammarians use different names. The ten Lakaras of
Pāṇini are according to their nomenclature, as follow:—वर्तति
(वर्ति:), अपवती, प्रस्तावी, परोक्षा, श्वस्तनी, भविष्यन्ती, वश्यमी, समयी,
क्रियातिपत्ति and आशीःर्—
'Apte's Guide'.
