§ 921. * With interrogatives the Present is often used in the 
sense of the Future when thought or desire is implied; कि करोषि 
i. e. (करिष्यामि), क गच्छामि i. e. (गमिष्यामि) What shall I do? 
Whither shall I go? एतयोः कतरं or एतयोः कतमं भोजयसि (i. e. भो-
जयिष्यसि or भोजयिष्यति) which of these persons will you feed? 
so कं तु पृच्छामि दुःखातः, &c.; but कः आमं गमिष्यति।

(a) It is also used in the sense of the Future in conditional 
sentences as implying a condition and the fulfilment of the 
desired object; योऽन्नं ददाति (दाता दास्यति वा) स स्वर्गं याति (याता 
यास्यति वा) he, who offers (or will offer) food, goes (or will go) 
to heaven i. e. if one offers &c.

§ 922. With the words यावत्, तावत् and others having a 
similar sense, the Present is sometimes used in the sense of the 
Future perfect; यावत्स त्वां न पश्यति तावत्समपसर before he sees 
you, move away (before he shall have seen you, &c.).

(b) † With the particles पुरा and यावत् the Present has the 
sense of the Future when certainty is indicated; यावयते साधयितुं 
त्वदर्थम्। (Rag.V. 25.) I will endeavour to accomplish your 
object. पुरा सप्तद्वीपां जयते वसुधाम् (S'ak. VII. 33) he will 
conquer the earth consisting of seven continents. पुरातुशेते तव 
बन्धने मनः। Kir. VIII. 8.
