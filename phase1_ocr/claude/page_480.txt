§ 790. When a past or potential passive participle is used
as predicate with a noun in apposition to the subject, the parti-
ciple agrees with the subject; छताः शरस्य कृतिषा तवासुरः (S'ak.
VI.) the demons are made the marks of your arrows by Hari.

CONCORD OF RELATIVE WITH ITS ANTECEDENT.

§ 791. The relative agrees with its antecedent in gender,
number and person, the cases of the relative and its antecedent
being determined by their relation to their respective clauses;
यस्यास्ति वित्तं स नरः कुलीनः he who has wealth has a noble family;
यस्य युध्यतेतं तस्य; येनैव युज्यते लोके युक्तस्तेन प्रयोजयेत् what, which
is fit to be united with anything, a wise man should unite
with it &c.

§ 792. When the relative has for its predicate a noun
differing in gender from the antecedent the relative generally
takes the gender of the antecedent noun, the demonstrative
pronoun following that of the noun it qualifies; परगुणासहिष्णुत्वं
हि यस्य दुर्जनतां स्वभावः-non-endurance of the merits of another is
but the nature of the wicked; शौर्यं हि यस्या प्रकृतिर्जेष्टसय.

§ 793. The relative pronoun यत् neu. sing. is used like the
English 'that' to introduce a clause, the gender of the demon-
strative pronoun being the same as that of the antecedent noun;
यहिद्वान् अपि नरः अन्यान् विभूणयते स धनमद एव it is the pride of
wealth that even a learned man slights another; उक्तोऽयं जनश्रवादः
यत्सत्पदैकपदमभ्युदयातिति it is a true saying that one good fortune
follows another.
