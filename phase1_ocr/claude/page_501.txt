§ 842. * When a word expressive of 'a debt,' is merely
intended to be stated as a cause, it is put in the Ablative case;
शताद्बद्धं द्रव्यं a thing mortgaged for a hundred rupees; ऋणाद्बद्धः
इव tied down as it were by the debt he owes.

§ 843. ( a ) The Ablative often denotes the cause of an action
or condition and may be translated by 'on account of, by reason
of,' &c.; मौनान्मूर्खः गण्यते one is considered a fool by reason of
his silence ( if he keeps silence ); गोमानुषाणां वधात् ( H. ) on
account of my killing cows and men.

( b ) The Ablative is used to make a causative assertion or to
advance an argument; धूमवतो वह्निमान् पर्वतः । The mountain is
fiery ( has fire on it ), because there is smoke; स्मृत्यनवकाशदोष-
प्रसङ्ग इति चेन्नान्यस्मृत्यनवकाशादोषप्रसङ्गात् ( Ved.Su. II. 1. 1 ) A
disputant says—if you say that our argument is liable to the
fault of giving no scope to your smṛitis then we reply: this your
argument will not hold; for in that case other smṛitis will
have no scope given to them.

( c ) The Ablative is used with comparatives or words having
a comparative sense; भक्तिमार्गात् ज्ञानमार्गः श्रेयान् the way of
knowledge is more efficient than that of devotion; अणोरण्वीयान्
smaller than an atom; अश्वमेधसहस्त्रेभ्यः सत्यमेवातिरिच्यते truth
alone is superior to a thousand horse-sacrifices; चैत्ररथादवरे not
inferior to Chaitraratḥa.
