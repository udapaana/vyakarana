THE PERFECT PARTICIPLES.

§ 895. The use of the perfect participles is very limited; it
is used in the sense of 'who or what has done &c.'; तं तस्मिन्विषये
नगरोपकण्ठे (Rag. V. 61.) him who had halted in the vicinity
of the city; येनासि सर्वाण्यभिजष्टुपुत्रे (Rag. V. 34.) of thee who
hast obtained all good things, स शुश्रुवांस्तद्वचनं (Bhaṭṭi. I. 20)
when he heard his words, &c.

THE PAST PASSIVE PARTICIPLES.

§ 896. The past passive participle is very frequently used to
supply the place of a verb, sometimes in conjunction with the
auxiliary verbs अस् and भू. The past passive participle agrees
adjectively with the object in gender, number and case, the
agent being put in the Instrumental, while the past active
participle is treated exactly like the verb in the past tense; तेन
कार्यं कृतं by him the work was done; तेन बन्धनानि छिन्नानि by him
the bonds were cut; आदिष्टास्मि देव्या धारिण्या I am commanded
by queen Dhāriṇī; स कार्यं कृतवान् he did the work; राम: देत्यान्
हतवान् Rāma killed the Rākshasas; कृतवत्यासि नावधीरणां thou
didst never despise me, &c.

§ 897. In the case of past passive participles of intransitive
roots the agent is put in the Nominative case; तदा प्रकृतितो राजा
रुरोद then the king of demons wept; सर्वं सुतोऽयं भाष: &c.

§ 898. The past passive participles are often used imperson-
ally, the agent being put in the Instrumental case; प्रभूतितं or
प्रद्योतितं सूर्येण it is shone by the sun; जितं पुत्रप्रेम्णा victorious (all
powerful) is the affection for sons; पण्डिताभितं तत्त्वधता he
showed his learning; प्रहृदितं or प्रमोदितं साधुना, &c.

§ 899. The past passive participles of the roots भू, कृष्
and पुष् and their synonyms are used in the sense of the Present
tense and are construed with the Genitive. (See § 857.)

For further particulars vide §§ 705-707.
