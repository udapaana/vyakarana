§ 916. The root अर्ह् 'to deserve' when used (in the second
person) in combination with the infinitive expresses 'a request,
a respectful entreaty,' and is generally, equivalent to the
English 'I pray, be pleased, &c.;' अग्निं शमयितुमर्हसि (Meg. 55)
please put out the fire; न चेद्रहस्यं प्रतिवक्तुमर्हसि (Kum. V. 40)
if you have nothing to conceal from me, please answer me;
द्वित्राणयहान्यर्हसि सोढुमध्य (Rag. V. 25) pray wait for two or three
days, O respectable one! &c. Sometimes it is equivalent to a
gentle command; त्वमं प्रसादयितुमर्हसि (Rag. I. 89) you ought to
please her; न तं शोचितुमर्हसि you ought not to bewail him. In
the third person and under the same circumstances it expresses
power or ability and can be translated by 'can;' कोऽन्यो भोक्तुमर्हति फाल्गुनात् Mah. Bhar. IV. 58. 27. देवं प्रज्ञाविशेषेण
को निवर्तितुमर्हति Ibid I. 1. 246.

§ 917. * The infinitive with the final म् dropped is joined with
the nouns काम and मनस् to form an adjectival compound mean-
ing 'wishing or having a mind to do any thing;' एतावदग्रतः
प्रतियातुकामं शिष्यं महर्षेः &c. (Rag. V. 18) the disciple of the
great sage was desirous of returning &c.; अयं जनः प्रष्टुमनास्त्वामेव
(Kum. V. 40) this person has a mind to ask you a question.
