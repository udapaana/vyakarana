THE MOODS.

THE IMPERATIVE MOOD.

§ 943. * The Imperative Mood does not express merely com-
mand, but also entreaty, benediction, courteous enquiry, gentle
advice, ability, &c.

( a ) In the second person this Mood is used to express com-
mand, entreaty, gentle advice and benedictions or blessings;
गच्छ ( त्वं ) कुसुमपुरं go to Kusumapura; परित्रायस्व परित्रायस्व help !
help ! क्षमस्वापराधं O God! forgive my faults, &c.; श्रुश्रूषस्व गुरून्
कुरु प्रियसखीवृत्तिं सपत्नीजने ( Śak. IV. ) serve your elders and treat
your co-wives as if they were your friends; एषि कार्यकरस्त्वं मे गच्छा
पृच्छ राघवं be thou my messenger, go to Rāghava, and say to
him; अनन्यभक्तं पतिमाप्नुहीति सा तथ्यमेवाभिहिता हरेण she was
addressed the truth by Hara when he said 'do thou obtain a
husband not devoted to any other lady.'

( b ) In the third person it is often used to express a blessing
and sometimes gentle command; विमुक्तां सिद्धिं नो...प्रकीर्णः पुष्पाणां
हरिचरणयोरञ्जलिर्बध्नु may this handful of flowers scattered on the
feet of Hari give us success; पर्जन्यः कालवर्षी भवतु may rain pour
down in time; पश्चात्तिष्ठन्तु भीताः शकटपतयः ( Mud. V. II. ).

( c ) In the first person it expresses a question, necessity, abi-
lity, &c.; किं करवाणि ते what should I do for you? अधुनाहं गच्छामि
I must go now; करवामेतत्तव देवि प्रियं तव we will ( are able to ) do
this thing, oh queen, which is agreeable to you; नहि प्रेष्यवधं घोरं
करवावस्त्व ते मतिः ( Bhaṭṭi XX. 6. ) let your thought be 'I must
not commit the horrible murder of an ambassador.'
