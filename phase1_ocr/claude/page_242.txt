स्म—is generally used as an expletive. Used with a form of
    the present tense of a verb, it gives in the sense of the
    past tense; as भवति स्म i.e. अभवत्. When used with
    the particle मा it has an intensive force; as मा स्म शोके
    मनः छुधाः &c.

चिद्—is added to कि and other indeclinables and asks a question
    or implies a doubt; किंचिद्, अहोचिद्, &c.

श्री—is used with कृ and its derivatives like a preposition in the
    sense of 'acceptance'; स्वीकारः, स्वीकृतम्, &c.

IV. CONJUNCTIONS.

§ 376. The following are the principal conjunctions in
Sanskrit:—

(a) Copulative अथ, अथो, उत, च, किंच, &c.
(b) Disjunctive वा, वा...वा, &c.
(c) Adversative अथवा, तु, किंतु, किंवा, &c.
(d) Conditional चेद्, यदि, यद्यपि, नेत्, नोचेत्, चेट् (used in
    sacrificial ceremonies), &c.
(e) Causal हि, तत्, तेन, &c.
(f) Interrogative आहो अहोचिद्, उत, उताहो, किं, किंनु,
    किंहत, किंचिद्, ननु, नखा, तु, &c.
(g) Affirmatives and negatives अद्ध, अथ किन्तु, आह्व, अद्धा &c.
(h) Conjunctions of time यावत्-तावत्, यदा, तदा &c.
(i) अथ and इति are used to mark the beginning and the close
    of a work respectively.
