(c) * दन्त necessarily when it is preceded by a numeral or स्व
indicating age, or when the whole compound forms a feminine
name, and optionally when it is preceded by श्याम, भसोड, a
word ending in अम्, छन्द, छम्न, दर्व, or चराह; द्वी दन्तो यस्य स द्विदन्त
'a child so small as to have two teeth only,' बड् दन्ता अस्य । पाडन्;
शोभना दन्ता अस्य छन्दर्, छदन्तो (i.e. youthful) fem; but द्विदन्त:
कती, सुदन्त: (having beautiful teeth) पृहर्व; अयोदन्ती, फलदन्ती (both
proper names,) &c. but समदन्ती having even rows of teeth;
श्यामा (black) दन्ता यस्य स शाम्बदन्-न्त; अरोकदन्-दन्त: having
black or thick-set teeth; कुमुडलाग्रदन्-न्त; having teeth like the
points of buds; शुभ्रदन्-न्त: &c.

(d) † कङ्कुद् 'when the compound denotes a particular stage
of growth;' अजातं कङ्कुद् यस्य स: अजातकङ्कुद् 'a bull that has not
got his hump as yet' i.e. 'who is very young'; also when it
is preceded by त्रि and the compound designates the mountain
of that name; as त्रिकङ्कुद् 'name of a mountain with three
peaks'; but त्रिकङ्कुद: 'having three humps'.

(e) § काकुद् 'the palate' (काकुदं तालु Kās.) necessarily when
preceded by उन् and त्रि and optionally when preceded by पृणी; as
उत्काकुद, त्रिकाकुद; पृणीकाकुद्-कुट:.

§ 258. †† हृदय is changed to हृद् when preceded by सु or दुस्
meaning 'a friend', or an 'enemy' respectively; शोभनं हृदयं यस्य
स सुहृद् 'a friend', दुहृद् 'an enemy'; but सुहृदय: 'one who has
got a good heart' i.e. 'one who is able to appreciate merits',
&c.; दुहृदय: 'a man of wicked intentions.'
