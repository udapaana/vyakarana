§ 832. * The object of roots implying motion ( i. e. the
place to which the motion is directed ), if it be not a road, is 
put in the Accusative or the Dative, when actual motion is
meant; ग्रामं ग्रामाय वा गच्छति 'goes to a village;' but मनसा हरिं 
ब्रजति, पन्थानं गच्छति.

                    THE ABLATIVE CASE.

§ 833. The principal sense of the Ablative case is अपादान् or
'motion away from †;' hence the noun from which the motion,
real or conceived, takes place is put in the Ablative case;
ग्रामादागच्छति 'comes from a village;' धावतोऽश्वादवतति 'falls down
from a running horse'; सदाचारात् प्रच्यवते.

(a) † Words having the sense of जुगुप्सा, विराम or 'cessation,'
'pause,' and प्रमाद 'swerving from,' also govern the Ablative
case; पापात् जुगुप्सते 'he hates sin;' न नवः प्रथुराफलोदयान्तिथः कर्मणः
विराम करोतिः ( Rag. VIII. 22. ) 'the new monarch, steady in
action, did not desist from efforts until they bore their fruit';
धर्मात्प्रमाद्यति 'swerves from duty'; स्वाधिकारप्रमत्तः ( Meg. I. )
'careless, failing in the discharge of the duties (of his office)',
so धर्मात्च्युवति; प्रमदोष्य निवर्तेत सर्वभोगस्य मक्षणात् ( Manu Smṛ.
V. 49. ), &c.

मद् with प्र in the sense of 'to be careless about' is used with
the Loc. also; अतोऽर्थेष प्रमाद्यन्ति प्रमदासु विपश्चितः । ( Manu Smṛ.
II. 213 ) 'on this account wise men are not careless about
their wives.'

§ 834. § In the case of words expressing fear or protection
from fear, that from which or whom the fear proceeds is put
