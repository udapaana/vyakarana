Note:—Though the root दा ought to govern the Dat. of the
indirect object, it is sometimes found used with the Gen. or the
Loc. राज्यं शिबीनां वृद्धं वै ददामि तव खेचर 'Oh sky-wanderer, I

प्रशिथिलीं दातुमिच्छसि 'thou who art thinking of bestowing the earth
upon Rāma,' &c.

§ 820. †The roots रुच् and others having the same sense
govern the Dative of the person or thing pleased or satisfied;
हरये रोचते भक्तिः 'Hari likes devotion;' अपां हि तृप्ताय न वारिधारा:
स्वादुः सुगन्धिः स्वदते तृप्ताय 'a stream of sweet water, cool and per-
fumed, is not liked by one who has already drunk enough of water.'

§ 821. ‡The roots श्लाघ् 'to praise,' गुह् 'to hide,' स्था 'to stand,'
and शप् 'to swear,' govern the Dative of the person to whom
some object or feeling is to be conveyed; गोपी स्मरात् कृष्णाय
श्लाघते-गुहते-तिष्ठते-शपते वा 'a cowherdess being incited by love
flatters Kṛṣṇa or apparently conceals her feeling from him
or waits for him or swears before him;' (Vide Bhatti, VII. 73.
74.); but राजानं श्लाघते मन्त्री 'a minister praises his king.'
